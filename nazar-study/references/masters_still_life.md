# The still-life and product lineage: what each master teaches

Product photography descends from four hundred years of still-life painting and a century of studio photography. Each entry: what they did, how, the practice we take, and where to study it. Tags: [SRC] checked against a museum or primary page (link given), [KNOWN] widely documented in art history (verify the detail before quoting it in client copy). Studied 2026-11-22. Never ask a model to imitate a living artist by name; describe the practice.

## Painters (the light came first)
| Master | What and how | The practice we take | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Juan Sánchez Cotán** (Spain, c.1600) | Fruit and vegetables in a stone window niche against black, some hanging on strings; one raking light from the left [KNOWN] | A **frame within the frame** and a dark void make ordinary produce monumental. One source, from one side, nothing in the background | San Diego Museum of Art, *Quince, Cabbage, Melon and Cucumber* |
| **Francisco de Zurbarán** (1633) | Objects in a single row on a table edge, raking light, black ground [KNOWN] | A **lineup on one baseline** with a strict rhythm reads like an altar, not a shelf. Our range "row" device descends from this | Norton Simon Museum, *Still Life with Lemons, Oranges and a Rose* |
| **Clara Peeters** (Antwerp, early 1600s) | Tiny self-portraits reflected in pewter and goblets [KNOWN] | **Reflections are content.** A reflection that shows the room (or a hidden detail) is a giggle that rewards a second look | Prado; Mauritshuis |
| **Willem Kalf** (1650s) | The sumptuous *pronkstilleven*: rare porcelain and silver emerging from darkness; light and shade make objects come out of the dark [SRC] | **Low key with one controlled pool of light;** let speculars on metal and glass carry the luxury, not props | Rijksmuseum SK-A-199: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-A-199 · Smarthistory video: https://smarthistory.org/?p=12404 |
| **Jean-Siméon Chardin** (1700s) | Humble kitchen objects, soft window light, broken paint texture [KNOWN] | **Dignity for the ordinary;** soft light and real surface texture beat polish | Louvre; NGA Washington |
| **Giorgio Morandi** (1900s) | The same bottles rearranged for decades, close tonal values, muted palette [KNOWN] | **A family as one tonal chord.** Small differences across a range read richer than loud differences | Museo Morandi, Bologna |

## Studio photographers (the product became the subject)
| Master | What and how | The practice we take | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paul Outerbridge**, *Ide Collar* (1922) | His first advertising assignment, published in *Vanity Fair*: a starched collar on a checkerboard; the circle breaks the grid. Duchamp reportedly pinned it up as a readymade [SRC] | **One product, one graphic idea.** A simple geometric ground makes the product's own shape the hero; advertising can be art if it is reduced | Met 265535: https://metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/265535 · MoMA 52457: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/52457 |
| **Edward Steichen** for J. Walter Thompson (from 1923) | Ran several photographic styles at once, each matched to the product's sales strategy: sentimental for Jergens lotion, fanciful for Pebeco toothpaste [SRC] | **The look follows the selling idea,** not the photographer's habit. Choose the visual register per line of a range on purpose | AIC Steichen archive: https://archive.artic.edu/steichen/conde-nast-1923-1937/index.html · P. Johnston, *Real Fantasies* |
| **Albert Renger-Patzsch**, *Die Welt ist schön* (1928) | 100 close-up photographs of objects, plants and industrial parts, cut from their commercial context; he wanted the title *Die Dinge* ("The Things") [SRC] | **The thing itself, close and frontal,** with its material truth. Our macro "detail" page is this practice | Stedelijk: https://stedelijk.nl/en/collection/68943-albert-renger-patzsch-die-welt-ist-schon · MoMA Object:Photo: https://www.moma.org/interactives/objectphoto/publications/780.html |
| **László Moholy-Nagy**, photograms (1920s Bauhaus) | Objects laid on photographic paper and exposed to light, no camera [SRC] | **Silhouette as identity.** If the pack's outline alone is recognisable, the shape is doing brand work | Met 265198; George Eastman Museum: https://collections.eastman.org/objects/280827/photogram |
| **Edward Weston**, *Pepper No. 30* (1930) | One pepper inside a tin funnel as a backdrop, natural light, long exposure [KNOWN] | **The backdrop shapes the light.** A curved reflector behind a small subject gives a glow with no visible source | Center for Creative Photography, Tucson |
| **Irving Penn** (1940s-2000s) | Vogue still lifes became the basis of later series; spare, elegant product images (cosmetics, food, liquor where the bottle and label were the focus); a master printer in black-and-white and colour [SRC] | **Spare, exact, printed well.** Every object earns its place; the label is lit to read; the print (our grade and export) is part of the work | AIC Irving Penn archives: https://archive.artic.edu/irvingpennarchives/overview · advertising: https://archive.artic.edu/irvingpennarchives/advertising |
| **Henry Leutwyler** (contemporary) | Treats still lifes as **portraits of objects**, often objects owned by famous people; 10 books with Steidl [SRC] | **Give the object a biography:** wear, a scratch, the evidence of use. A pack that has been handled reads more real than a mint render | Graphis: https://blog.graphis.com/?p=21843 · Tings: https://tingsmagazine.com/article/life-edited-henry-leutwyler |
| **Carl Kleiner** (with stylist Evelina Kleiner), IKEA cookbook *Homemade Is Best* (2010) | Raw ingredients arranged as exact graphic compositions, top-down, on flat colour [KNOWN] | **Flat-lay as typography.** Top-down product groups on a flat brand colour can be as designed as a layout; spacing is the content | IKEA / the photographer's own site |
| **Thomas Demand** (contemporary) | Builds life-size paper models of scenes, photographs them, destroys the models [KNOWN] | **Constructed realism:** a built set can read as a document if the light is right. Our composites are constructed; the light must be documentary | Museum collections (MoMA, Tate) |
| **Liquids and high-speed** (specialist studios) | Practitioner lists name specialists who work almost only with liquids [3P: greatbigphotographyworld.com] | Splash shots are a craft of their own (strobe duration, real pours). **Never fake one with AI** (`../../nazar-shoot/references/scenes.md`) | |

## Patterns across the lineage (the rules we take)
1. **One source, placed for the object's form,** with fill controlled by a card or the room. Two equal lights cancel the form. Every master above works with one dominant source.
2. **The ground is part of the idea:** void (Cotán, Kalf), graphic pattern (Outerbridge), flat colour (Kleiner), real surface (Chardin). Choose it on purpose, never as default grey.
3. **Reduce until the product's own shape carries it** (Outerbridge, Moholy-Nagy, Penn).
4. **Material truth beats polish:** texture, wear, a crease (Chardin, Renger-Patzsch, Leutwyler).
5. **A range is a chord:** small, controlled differences (Morandi); a lineup on one baseline (Zurbarán).
6. **Reflections are a second picture** that can hold a hidden detail (Peeters); keep them physically right (`../../nazar-shoot/engine/shotplan.py`).
7. **The look follows the selling idea** (Steichen): decide the register per line, then hold it across every SKU (all-or-none).

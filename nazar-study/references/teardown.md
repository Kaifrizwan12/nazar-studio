# Teardowns: back-engineering a still, a scene, a pack shot, a case study

A teardown answers "how was this made, and what do we take?" in a fixed order, so two people tearing down the same frame land on the same recipe. Every teardown ends in a recipe card (`../templates/recipe_card.md`).

## 1. A still (photograph or painting)
1. **Measure:** `look.py card ref.jpg`. Copy the words and the five numbers that matter most for this job (usually black point, contrast, saturation, falloff, empty space).
2. **Light:** run the eight clues (`reading_light.md`). Draw the top view: camera, subject, every source with its size and height. If you can't place a source, say "unknown", don't guess.
3. **Lens and camera:** perspective (do verticals converge?), compression (does the background look close and large = long lens, or far and small = wide?), depth of field (how fast it goes soft behind), camera height relative to the subject.
4. **Composition:** where the weight sits (`weight_centroid`), what is in front of, at and behind the subject (layers), what is cut by the frame edge and why, the empty space and where it is.
5. **Surface and styling:** materials, wear, the one thing that breaks perfection (a crumb, a crease, a drip, a fingerprint). Real photographs always have one; generated ones rarely do on purpose.
6. **Grade:** shadow and highlight colour lean, grain, halation, vignette.
7. **The one sentence:** what it does that ours should do.

## 2. A film scene (for cinematic light and sequence)
1. Pick 3-5 frames from the scene: a wide, a medium, a close-up, the most extreme light.
2. Tear down each frame as a still, then note what **stays the same** across them (the motivated source: a window, a lamp, a fire) and what changes (the angle, so the light is cheated per shot).
3. Note the motivation: what in the story or set justifies each source. Product translation: every light in a product scene needs a source the viewer would believe (`cinema_light.md`).
4. Cite the film, the scene and the cinematographer. Never put film stills in a deliverable.

## 3. A Behance or award case study (structure, not tone)
Do this for 6-10 strong projects in the same category (featured or award-winning packaging, identity or campaign work) before writing a flatplan. Fill one row each:

| Field | How to read it |
|---|---|
| Panels | Count them. Note the cover (product, type, or scene?) and whether panel 1 repeats it |
| Beat order | Hook / brief / insight / idea / system / applications / payoff / credits. Mark which beats exist and in what order |
| First two panels | What they say in 5 seconds. Is the idea stated before any system page? |
| Devices | One name per panel (`../../nazar-story/references/sequence.md` device list). Count repeats |
| Text share | Rough share of panels with more than one sentence; words on the busiest panel |
| Scale moves | Where they go macro, where everything is together, where something bleeds off the page |
| Ground colour rhythm | How often the background colour changes |
| Process shown | Sketches, research, rejected routes, the dieline, print proofs: which, and where in the order |
| Real-world moment | Photo, film, in-hand, shelf, or none; how it is labelled if it is a render |
| Close | Credits, studio page, thank-you; what the last image mirrors |
| Measured look | `look.py set` on its panels: palette, empty space, text density |

Then write: the 5 moves that recur across the strongest, the 3 that recur in weak ones (template-for-every-panel, cartoon mockups, callouts over the product), and the gap our story can own. Feed it to `../../nazar-story/references/case_study_playbook.md`.

## 4. A pack shot (what pro product photographers do)
Look for these, in this order, on 6-20 strong pack shots in the category:
- **How the print is protected:** where the glare would land on glossy or foil areas and how it was kept off the type (light moved outside the family of angles, a gradient placed on purpose on an unprinted area, a polariser on dielectric gloss).
- **How form is shown:** a single gradient across the face (a large source at a raking angle) vs flat front light (reads like a scan).
- **How the edge is defined:** a rim, a dark edge from a black flag (negative fill), or a light edge against a dark ground.
- **How it sits:** contact shadow, cast shadow, reflection, a surface with texture.
- **What is around it:** props that tell where it lives, or nothing.
- **What is imperfect:** creases, a slightly open zip, a crumb.

## Recipe card fields (summary)
Source and rights · job it serves · measured numbers (5) · light top view (sources, sizes, heights, ratios) · lens, camera height, depth · composition (weight, layers, empty space) · surface and the one imperfection · grade · the one sentence · what we refuse from it.

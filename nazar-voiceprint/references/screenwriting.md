# Script and creative-narrative language

Confidence tags: [V] checked at the source, [P] practitioner/trade analysis, [UNVERIFIED].

## 1. Dialogue signature: what's real
- **Computational work exists:**
  - A 2020 *Digital Humanities Quarterly* study (vol. 14.4) built a corpus of roughly 280–300 film dialogues and applied most-frequent-word stylometry and sentiment analysis, labelled by screenwriter and date [V].
  - Buckland extended Burrows's Delta to screenplay dialogue and tested it on the three brothers in *The Darjeeling Limited* [V].
  - Bootstrap character-distinctiveness methods have been applied to the DraCor drama corpora (arXiv 2301.05659) [V].
  - Dialogue authorship has been modelled with word2vec and character n-grams (ACL SIGHUM 2019, W19-2504) [V].
- **Named auteur signatures are practitioner analysis, not scholarship.**
  - ScriptMag groups Sorkin, Mamet and Milch under "paired construction" in dialogue [P].
  - Sorkin's own craft term is "cadence": rapid-fire exposition during physical movement [P].
  - Use these as vocabulary, not evidence.

## 2. Structural baseline (a ruler, never a prescription)
- **Save the Cat's 15 beats (Blake Snyder, 2005), in order** [V names/order]: Opening Image · Theme Stated · Set-Up · Catalyst · Debate · Break into Two · B Story · Fun and Games · Midpoint · Bad Guys Close In · All Is Lost · Dark Night of the Soul · Break into Three · Finale · Final Image.
- **Page placements against Snyder's 110-page model are approximate, and sources disagree.** Roughly: Opening Image p.1, Theme Stated ~p.5, Catalyst ~p.12, Bad Guys Close In ~pp.50–75, Final Image ~pp.110–120. **Express a person's habits as percentages of their own script length, not page numbers.**
- **Syd Field (*Screenplay*, 1979) and McKee (*Story*, 1997)** supply standard, uncontested vocabulary: inciting incident, progressive complications, midpoint, crisis, climax, resolution.
- **Monologues and short pieces:** the beat sheet is for features. For a monologue, scene or reel, map the internal *turns* instead: setup → complication → reversal → button.

## 3. Script-format ratios (measurable today)
- Real tools already chart these:
  - **OpenDraft:** scene-length distribution and per-scene dialogue/action density.
  - **Highland 3:** a readability score against page, scene and ratio norms.
  - **Starc:** description vs. dialogue proportions.
  - **WriterDuet:** compares action lines to dialogue lines, and says outright that **there is no industry-standard ratio** [V].
- An analytics firm cites *Beauty and the Beast* at 63% action / 37% dialogue and *American Hustle* at 30% / 70% [P; primary source uncited]. It shows the range, not a norm.
- No tool tracks parenthetical ("wryly") frequency as a named metric. That's a real gap the qualitative read covers.

## 4. Reel / short-form pacing (use the creator's own numbers)
- **No official TikTok or Meta document confirms a "first 3 seconds" rule.** These claims trace back to marketing blogs relaying unsourced quotes [V that it's unsourced].
- **What *is* real:** TikTok analytics expose a 3-second retention percentage ("hook rate"). The metric exists even where the "rule" is folklore.
- **MrBeast:** no primary documentation exists. The most citable line is secondhand, from his former retention strategist Mario Joos: *"It's not the cutting speed, it's the speed at which you introduce progressive novel information"* [P, via vidpros.com]. Cut-interval figures conflict across sources (2–4 s vs. 1.1 s) [UNVERIFIED].
- **Rule:** measure the creator's own past reels — where the hook lands, how often new information arrives, how they end (CTA, trail-off, loop) — and use that as the baseline. Never quote an industry number.

## 5. Stability across projects
- No study tests whether one screenwriter's dialogue style holds across genres. The DHQ article treats it as open and points to Kozloff, Veirano Pinto (2014) and Jaeckle's *Film Dialogue* (2013).
- What *is* documented: **genre itself drives measurable dialogue features** (screwball, melodrama, western, sitcom and procedural each have markers).
- So a "signature" pulled from one genre may just be the genre. Test each feature across the person's own projects, by analogy with the prose finding [inference].

## Signal types for a script / creative-narrative profile
1. **Dialogue cadence:** line length, interruption and overlap, repetition and paired construction.
2. **Structural turns:** where reversals land as a % of the piece, compared with the baseline.
3. **Format ratios:** action/dialogue density, scene length, use of parentheticals.
4. **Devices:** refrains, motifs, code-switching, direct address, frame breaks (e.g. "the story's ending is bad, let's change it").
5. **Reel pacing:** hook placement, information-reveal rate, ending style, all measured from their own catalogue.
6. **Stability across their own projects:** tested per feature, never assumed.

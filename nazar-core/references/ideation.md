# Ideation: how great concepts are actually made

Process steps don't produce ideas. Thinking does. This file collects the **concept methods** behind work that lasted, and how to run them. Use it in every kit at the "routes" stage. Each idea needs a single sentence of meaning, or it's decoration.

## 1. Principles from the people who did it
| Principle | Source | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum meaning, minimum means** | Abram Games (his motto) [SRC] | Say one thing with the fewest elements. Games often fused two familiar images into a new third one. |
| **The problem is the problem** | Bob Gill, *Forget All the Rules…* [SRC] | Reframe the brief until it's interesting. A dull brief gives dull work, so rewrite the problem before drawing. "Think first, then draw." "Interesting words need boring graphics." |
| **Symbolize and summarize** | Saul Bass [SRC] | Reduce a whole story to one image. Strip every non-essential line. |
| **A logo designates; it doesn't need to depict** | Paul Rand, 1991 [ARCH] | Meaning grows into a mark over time. Don't illustrate the product. |
| **Simple, yet distinctive** | Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv, *Identify* [SRC] | The daily tension: simple shapes that still belong to only one brand. |
| **Look sideways** | Alan Fletcher, *The Art of Looking Sideways* [SRC] | Insight comes from connecting unrelated things. Collect widely and notice everything. |
| **Visual wit** | Woody Pirtle, Pentagram [SRC] | A visual pun that rewards discovery ("witty and economical"). The joke must carry the brand's meaning. |
| **Speed is earned** | Paula Scher's Citi sketch, drawn in a first meeting [SRC] | A fast idea comes from years of looking. Don't confuse a quick sketch with a quick job: the system and refinement come after. |
| **Discovery happens while refining** | The FedEx arrow, found during letter-spacing exploration of hundreds of versions [SRC] | Ideas also appear in the craft stage. Keep looking at negative space, spacing and accidents while refining. |
| **A rebus** | Glaser's I ♥ NY: a heart replaces a word [SRC] | Replace a word with a picture (or a picture with a letter). It's instantly shareable. |
| **Meaning by structure** | Stankowski's slash in a square = "growth in a stable environment" [SRC] | Two abstract elements in a relationship can carry a whole strategy. |
| **Many parts, one shape** | Wolff Olins' Unilever U, made of 25 icons [SRC] | A big organisation's breadth can be shown as small parts forming one form. |
| **Strategy and design in parallel** | Michael Johnson, johnson banks: the "half step" between strategy and design [SRC] | Run narrative and visual routes side by side. A visual can rewrite the strategy. |

## 2. The idea generators (run several per job)
**A. Reframe the brief** (Gill). Write the problem 5 ways: literally, as the customer would say it, as the opposite, as a promise, and as a question. Pick the most interesting framing, then design for that.

**B. Word → image mapping.** List 30 words around the brand idea (nouns, verbs, places, sensations, local words). Then list 30 images for them. Look for **collisions**: two images that fuse into a new third one (Games's method).

**C. The name holds the mark.** Write and draw the name 50 times in different hands and faces. Look for a hidden shape, a letter that can become an object, a diacritic or dot, a ligature, or a space between letters (the FedEx arrow).

**D. Semiotic choice** (Peirce) [SRC]. Decide deliberately what kind of sign the mark is:
- **Icon:** it resembles the thing (a bird for an airline). Instant, but literal.
- **Index:** it points to the thing by cause or trace (a footprint, smoke, a stamp, a map pin). Often the most interesting.
- **Symbol:** learned by convention (an abstract shape, a letter). Needs time, but is most ownable.
Most strong marks combine them. Choose on purpose.

**E. Random entry** (Edward de Bono) [SRC]. Pick a genuinely random noun, list 5 forced connections to the brief, and develop the strongest. It breaks fixation.

**F. Provocation (PO)** (de Bono) [SRC]. Make an impossible statement ("the logo has no shape", "the packaging is invisible", "the brand only speaks at night"). Then follow it for its *movement*, towards something workable. Expect most provocations to fail; that's the point.

**G. SCAMPER** (Eberle) [SRC]. On a candidate idea: Substitute · Combine · Adapt · Modify/Magnify · Put to another use · Eliminate · Reverse. Good for developing an existing idea, not for starting from nothing.

**H. Morphological matrix** [SRC]. Rows are parameters (shape, construction, metaphor, colour logic, behaviour) and columns are options. Combine across rows to find unexpected pairings. Pair it with SCAMPER to transform the results.

**I. Synectics / analogy** (Gordon & Prince) [SRC]. "How would nature / a city / a kitchen / a machine solve this?" Find a parallel world, solve it there, and bring the answer back.

**J. Oblique Strategies** (Eno & Schmidt) [SRC]. When stuck, draw a card-style prompt ("Honour thy error as a hidden intention", "What would your closest friend do?") and apply it literally.

**K. Behaviour first.** Ask what the identity *does* (opens, fills, counts, travels, blinks, syncs) before what it *looks like*. Behaviours make flexible systems and better motion (Wolff Olins' idea-as-a-verb).

**L. Constraint games.** Design it with one shape only, in one colour, from type only, as a rubber stamp, as an embroidery, as a sound. Constraints expose the core.

**M. Archive callback.** Take a forgotten form from the category's archive (an old stamp, a label convention, a trade mark) and make it contemporary. It gives the work lineage, and it's distinctive because competitors forgot it.

## 3. Running a concept session
1. Restate the reframed problem and the brand idea at the top of the page.
2. Run at least **3 generators** (one verbal, one visual, one provocative). Timebox each.
3. Go for **quantity**: 50–100 thumbnails or one-line ideas. Don't judge yet.
4. Cluster them into **territories** (3–5). Name each territory and write its meaning in one line.
5. Judge against: meaning (does it carry the idea?), distinctiveness (does only this brand own it?), simplicity (can a child draw it from memory?), system potential (does it extend to patterns, motion, voice?) and craft potential.
6. Develop **3 routes**: one safe-but-sharp, one bold, one unorthodox. Each goes to the same finish.
7. Write each route's story in **one sentence**. If it needs a paragraph, the idea isn't clear yet.

## 4. Kill list (reject these)
- The category cliché (leaf for natural, globe for international, swoosh for movement, lightbulb for ideas, a handshake for trust)
- Ideas that need explaining before they're seen
- Meaning that only the designer knows about, and that the work doesn't show
- Effects carrying the idea (gradients, bevels, shadows): the idea must survive in flat black
- Trend-of-the-year forms that will date in 3 years
- Anything resembling a competitor's or a famous mark

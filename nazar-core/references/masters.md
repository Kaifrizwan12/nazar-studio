# What the masters actually do

Practices drawn from the most respected identity designers and the research behind brand recognition. Each practice is tagged and paired with **how to apply it** in a Kaif book. Where secondary sources disagree, the safer reading is used. Treat these as criteria for judging work, not rules to copy (that's how Dieter Rams framed his own principles).

## Thinking and process
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Understand before designing** (semantics → syntactics → pragmatics) | Vignelli, *The Vignelli Canon* [KNOWN] | Research first. Every element must mean something, be structured correctly, and be understood by its user. Run the three tests on every page. |
| **One big idea, then the look** | Common to Pentagram and Wolff Olins identity work [SRC] | Lock the idea in one sentence before any visual exploration (intake Q5). |
| **The idea is a verb, not an object** | Wolff Olins, London 2012 (an identity built to flex across uses) [SRC] | Describe what the identity *does* (syncs, ripens, travels), not only what it *is*. A system built on a behaviour flexes better. |
| **Partner-led, hands-on** | Pentagram's model: practising designers lead their own projects [SRC] | Kaif stays the author. Claude proposes and builds, but every concept carries his judgement. Never present a route he hasn't chosen. |
| **As little design as possible** | Rams's ten principles ("less, but better") [KNOWN] | Cut every element that doesn't do a job. When a page feels weak, remove before adding. |
| **Criteria, not dogma** | Rams; Natasha Jen's critique of rigid 5-step process models [SRC] | Use these files as judgement, not a recipe. If the brand needs something else, do that and note why in `SPEC.md`. |

## Logo
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **A logo designates; it needn't depict** | Paul Rand, "Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons" (1991) [ARCH] | Don't force the product into the mark. Its meaning comes from the brand's quality over time. |
| **Simple, distinctive, adaptable** | Rand [ARCH] | Test it at 16 px, in one colour, reversed, blurred, cut in vinyl and embroidered. A fussy mark has a built-in weakness. |
| **The blur test** | Attributed to Rand's process [SRC] | Blur the mark heavily. If it's still recognisable, it has a strong silhouette. Show this test on the logo anatomy page. |
| **Distinctive beats different** | Byron Sharp & Jenni Romaniuk, *How Brands Grow Part 2*, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* [SRC] | The goal is fast recognition and correct attribution to the brand, not novelty. Keep assets consistent for years. |
| **Fame × uniqueness** | Romaniuk's test for a distinctive asset [SRC] | For each asset (colour, shape, character, pattern), ask whether people will know it and whether it points to *this* brand only. Drop assets that fail. |

## Grid and structure
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **The grid as the book's spine** | Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* [KNOWN] | A modular grid (columns + rows) built on a baseline unit taken from body leading. Every element snaps to it. |
| **Grid sheets for every format** | Wim Crouwel, Total Design / Stedelijk [SRC] | Make a grid template for each application family (poster, post, pouch) and show it on the spec sheet. |
| **Systems generate the parts** | Otl Aicher, Munich 1972: orthogonal + diagonal grid generating 150+ pictograms, a deliberately limited colour palette [SRC] | Build icons, patterns and the atom from one construction grid, and show it. Choosing colours by what you *leave out* is a valid palette strategy. |
| **…but the eye has the final word** | Mark Holt found gaps between Aicher's generative grid and the finished forms [SRC] | Correct optically after construction (overshoots, optical centring, weight balance). Note the corrections on the anatomy page. |
| **Type built from the system** | Crouwel's New Alphabet (a 5×7 grid for early screens) [SRC] | A custom display face or number set from the brand's grid can be the most ownable asset. Propose one when the budget allows. |

## Typography
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Few typefaces, used well** | Vignelli's modernist discipline [KNOWN] | 2–3 families. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space, not from more fonts. |
| **Comfortable measure** | Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*: about 45–75 characters, 66 ideal; narrower for multiple columns [SRC] | Cap text blocks by characters, not by page width. The engine's `para()` takes a max width, so derive it from the measure. |
| **Leading follows measure** | General typographic practice [KNOWN] | Wider lines need more leading. Use 1.3–1.45 for text, tighter for display. |
| **Text on a baseline rhythm** | Müller-Brockmann, Bringhurst [KNOWN] | Spacing between blocks in multiples of the baseline unit. |

## Colour
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour is relative** | Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color* [KNOWN] | Never approve a swatch alone. Approve it on its real neighbours and real substrate. |
| **Contrast of extension** | Johannes Itten [KNOWN] | Balance by area: bright colours need less space. Write the shares into the rules. |
| **Colour as a distinctive asset** | Sharp, Romaniuk [SRC] | One ownable colour, used consistently on every touchpoint, beats a rich palette used loosely. |
| **A limited palette from a historic system** | Wada's combination dictionary; Werner's nomenclature; riso and two-colour print [ARCH] | Start from a documented combination and credit it. |

## Texture
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Texture from real surfaces** | Common practice among print-led designers [SRC] | Scan real paper, ink, walls and packaging (own scans or licensed ones), not generic packs. Note the source in `research/`. |
| **Halftone is easy to do badly** | Designers' guidance on halftone overlays [SRC] | Small dots, high contrast, one screen angle with a reason (taken from the logo or a period press). Never mix screen angles randomly. |
| **Grain stays below notice** | Designer guidance; e.g. paper grain in Google's *The A-Z of AI* [SRC] | Grain should be felt, not seen: about 2–6% noise. Readability wins. |
| **Native resolution, seamless tiles** | Texture practice [SRC] | Use scans at native resolution, build tiles that hide their seams, and never upscale a texture for print. |
| **Riso logic, digitally** | Riso studios: translucent spot inks, 0–3 mm shift between passes, overprint order changes colour [SRC] | To fake riso: separate the layers by colour, multiply-blend them, offset 1–3 px, and don't align textures across layers. In real riso, avoid tight registration and large dark solids. |
| **Each texture carries a meaning** | Kaif practice | At most two textures, each tied to one side of the brand's tension. |

## Packaging and shelf
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Visibility drives sales from shelf** | Pack-testing research (Metrixlab and others) [SRC] | Test the pack at shelf distance and size, among competitors, in greyscale. The brand and variant must read in under 2 seconds. |
| **Brand block + variant clarity** | Metrixlab guidance [SRC] | A consistent brand block across the range, and variant cues (colour, image) that don't break it. |
| **Don't throw away distinctive assets** | Tropicana's 2009 redesign, reportedly reverted after a large sales drop [SRC, anecdotal] | When refreshing an existing brand, list its existing assets and keep the ones people recognise. |
| **The product is the hero** | Common in strong food packaging [KNOWN] | Use windows, real colour and the real form. Don't hide the product behind graphics. |

## The guideline itself
| Practice | Who / where | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **The manual as an object people want** | NASA 1975 and NYCTA 1970 reissues became collector books [SRC] | Design the book so the client is proud to show it: a real narrative, a beautiful grid, a cover worth keeping. |
| **Teach people to make new things** | Mailchimp's fictitious-brand examples [SRC] | Include one page showing the system applied to something *not* in the book, so teams learn the logic, not just the templates. |
| **Personality rules in writing** | Mailchimp (Freddie never speaks); Duolingo (Duo always in character) [SRC] | Write the "never" list for any character or voice. |
| **Show, then tell** | Universal in great manuals [KNOWN] | A large correct example, then one line of rule. |

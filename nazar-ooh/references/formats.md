# Formats and adaptation

## Common formats (the media owner's spec always wins)
| Format | Context | Design notes |
|---|---|---|
| Roadside billboard / hoarding | Drivers at speed; 1–5 s viewing | The shortest copy, the biggest type, the brand block large and placed consistently |
| Transit shelter / street poster (e.g. 6-sheet) | Pedestrians, people waiting | More copy is possible; eye level; QR works here |
| Wallscape / mural | Long dwell, landmark | It can be art; hand-painted versions earn affection and media |
| Bus side and back, taxi, rickshaw, delivery bag | Moving and stopped | The back is read by the vehicle behind (stopped traffic gives time); avoid windows, handles and lights; design for curves and seams |
| Digital screens (DOOH) | Rotating slots, often 6–15 s, often silent | Motion in loops; brand visible in every frame; no small text; respect the brightness and content rules |
| Airport, mall, metro | Dwell time, a premium audience | Sequences along a walking route; wayfinding-style placement |
| In-venue / event / stage | A captive, social audience | Designed for phone photos (backdrops, stage screens, step-and-repeat walls) |
| Shop signage / fascia | Daily, local | Readable from across the street and day and night (lit vs unlit) |

## Adapting one idea across a plan
1. Design the **hero** format first (usually the hardest: the roadside board).
2. Define what's fixed: the idea, the image crop logic, the brand block, the type hierarchy.
3. Re-compose, don't scale. Each aspect ratio gets its own layout (wide, tall, square, tiny).
4. Simplify for speed: fewer words on fast media, more detail where people wait.
5. Keep a **master layout sheet** showing every format side by side at the same visual scale.
6. For DOOH, make a 6–15 s loop where the brand is present throughout, plus a still fallback.

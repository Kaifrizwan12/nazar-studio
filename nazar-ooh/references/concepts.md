# Concepts for outdoor

## What great outdoor does
- **One thought, instantly.** A strong image plus a few words, readable in a glance.
- **Uses its context.** The site, the weather, the time, the street name, the neighbouring billboard, the traffic jam: the board responds to where it is.
- **Surprises.** It breaks the rectangle (extensions, cut-outs, 3D), uses the real world as part of the ad, or changes over time.
- **Rewards a second look** for people who pass daily (a detail, a series that evolves).
- **Earns media:** people photograph it, share it, talk about it, and the press covers it.

## Idea patterns
| Pattern | How |
|---|---|
| **Contextual** | The copy talks about where it is ("You've been stuck here 12 minutes. Our coffee takes 2.") |
| **Serial / evolving** | A sequence across sites or days that tells a story or counts down |
| **Real-time (DOOH)** | Content changes with the time, weather, score or traffic |
| **Break the frame** | Extensions, cut-outs, 3D props, shadows that move with the sun |
| **Interaction** | Something to do: scan, photograph, take, sit, find |
| **Reversal / pun** | A local phrase turned, a visual pun on the site or object |
| **Scale shock** | A product or detail at enormous scale; a giant version of something small |
| **Vehicle-native** | Taxi, bus or rickshaw backs that talk to the car behind them |
| **Cinematic** | The board as a film poster or title card, letterboxed; a "now showing" grammar |
| **Topical** | A fast reaction to culture (needs fast approvals and print or digital turnaround) |

## Street to screen (every idea needs its twin)
- **The photo people take:** design a moment worth photographing (a pose spot, a mirror, a sign with their area's name).
- **A reel:** the board's story continues online (a behind-the-scenes install, a reveal, a series).
- **Geo-targeting:** the same message reaches phones in the area at the same time.
- **A QR or code worth scanning:** a real reward (a deal, a game, a map, content), with a short URL as a fallback. Scan-test it at distance.
- Measure what you can (scans, mentions, searches, footfall), without making counting the point.

## Guerrilla and stunts: the rules
- Legal (permits obtained), safe (no obstruction, no distraction for drivers), and respectful (no damage, no offence to communities or faith, no fake emergencies).
- Have a clean-up and removal plan.
- The client signs off on the risk in writing.
- Prefer clever over loud. The best stunts feel like a gift to the street, not a nuisance.

## FOOH (faux out-of-home: digital fake-OOH videos)
If proposed, label it honestly as CGI. Never present it as a real installation, and never use AI-generated "fake" real places in ways that could mislead.

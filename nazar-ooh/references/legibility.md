# Legibility at a distance

## Letter height vs viewing distance (rules of thumb that disagree; test it)
| Guideline | Ratio | At 150 m (~500 ft) |
|---|---|---|
| Common commercial rule | ~1 inch of letter height per 10 ft (≈ 2.5 cm per 3 m) | ≈ 1.27 m |
| US Sign Council / FHWA-style (more conservative for reading) | ~1 inch of cap height per 30 ft (≈ 2.5 cm per 9 m) | ≈ 42 cm minimum |
Sources disagree, so choose the more generous size for the main line, and test [SRC]. Speed matters: faster roads need bigger and fewer words. Some transport authorities publish letter heights by travel speed.

## What changes legibility [SRC]
Letter height · stroke weight (not too thin) · letter spacing (slightly open at distance) · contrast (light/dark difference matters more than hue) · mixed case is generally easier to read than ALL CAPS · approach angle · illumination (day and night) · weather and dust · occlusion (trees, poles, traffic) · the time available.

## Rules
- About 6–7 words for fast roadside boards (one breath) [SRC].
- One focal image, high contrast, simple shapes. Avoid thin, condensed or script faces for key words.
- Brand block large enough to read at the same distance as the headline.
- No small print except the legally required (and make even that readable where people stop).
- Night: check lit vs unlit colours; dark boards with light type often read better under lights; test for halation.
- QR codes only where people stop or walk, and big enough to scan from the expected distance.

## Testing
1. **Thumbnail test:** shrink the layout to a few cm on screen, or view it across the room. If you can't read it, nobody at speed can.
2. **Distance simulation:** print an A4 version and view it from a distance scaled to the real site ratio.
3. **Site photo composite:** place the design on a real photo of the site, at the right angle and scale.
4. **Day/night and greyscale checks.**
5. If possible, a real-world drive-by or walk-by with the client before final approval.

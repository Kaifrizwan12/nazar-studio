# The pack system

## 1. Range architecture (what's fixed, what flexes)
The pack system is a rulebook: what stays constant across every SKU, and what each variant changes [SRC].
- **Fixed (the brand block):** logo position and size, primary brand colour or structure, type system, layout grid, and the key distinctive asset.
- **Flex (the variant cue):** one dimension only: colour, illustration or image, a typographic name, or the structure itself.
- **Plan for the 2-year range.** Map the colour spectrum (or image set) for every current and planned variant before launch, so colour 12 doesn't collide with colour 3 [SRC].
- Sub-ranges (premium, kids, seasonal) get one extra signal (a band, a finish, a badge) on top of the core system.
- Test the architecture across the **full** planned range on one sheet, not just the first SKU [SRC].

## 2. Front-of-pack hierarchy
Shoppers typically read in this order: **brand → product name → variant → key benefit → the rest** [SRC].
- Brand block: recognisable from 2–3 m.
- Product name and variant: clear in a glance.
- **One** key benefit or claim. Cramming the front is a common mistake [SRC].
- Mandatory front items (as the market requires: net quantity, sometimes the product name and legal descriptor).
- The window or product image, where the product is the hero.
Squint test: only the brand block and the variant should survive.

## 3. Variant systems (choose one as the lead)
| System | How | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Colour-coded | Each variant has its own colour field | Running out of distinct colours; category colour codes (e.g. a colour that already means "light") |
| Image-coded | Each variant has its own illustration or photo | Keeping a consistent style; illustrator continuity |
| Typographic | The variant name is the hero | It needs strong type and a short name |
| Structure-coded | A different shape or format per variant | Cost and tooling |
| Pattern-coded | Each variant has its own pattern from one system | Readability at a distance |

## 4. Back and side panels
- A **back-of-pack story**: one short paragraph or a diagram (origin, process, how to use), in the brand's voice. This is where the giggle and the details live.
- **Mandatory information** (varies by market; the client supplies exact text and is legally responsible): product name and descriptor, ingredients, allergens, net quantity, nutrition facts, manufacturer or packer name and address, country of origin, batch or lot, manufacture and expiry or best-before dates, storage and usage instructions, certification marks only if held, recycling marks.
- **A variable-data zone:** a blank, light area for inkjet or stamped batch numbers and dates. Ask the converter for its position and size.
- **Barcode** (GS1 EAN-13 or UPC-A): print at 100% nominal where possible (about 37.3 × 25.9 mm for EAN-13; GS1 allows roughly 80–200% magnification depending on scanning). Keep the left and right quiet zones clear (the left is wider). Dark bars on a light background, and preferably black on white. Never truncate the height, or place it over folds, seals or curves. Always scan-test the proof [SRC].
- Languages: plan space for every language required, so text never shrinks below the legal minimum size.
- QR codes: give them a purpose (a recipe, origin, a reorder link), with a short URL fallback and a scan test.

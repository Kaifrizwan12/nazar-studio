# Structure and substrate (decide with the converter)

## Common formats
| Format | Typical construction | Design notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stand-up pouch | Laminated films or paper + film (e.g. kraft/PET/PE), zipper, tear notch | Seals (often 8–12 mm) carry no fine detail; the gusset shows the base; a window shows the product |
| Flat pouch / sachet | Laminated film | Small faces: a ruthless hierarchy |
| Folding carton | Board (SBS, recycled), offset printed, die-cut and glued | Grain direction, crease lines, a glue flap with no ink in the glue area, tuck or crash-lock base |
| Rigid gift box | Board wrapped in printed paper | Wrap tolerances; inside print for the unboxing moment |
| Bottle / jar + label | Glass or plastic; paper or film labels | Curvature distortion; the label-to-contents colour relationship; shrink sleeves distort artwork |
| Tin | Printed metal | Metal printing conditions; white base coat needed for bright colours |
| Corrugated shipper / mailer | Corrugated board, flexo or digital | Coarser print; unboxing inside; e-commerce dimensions |

## Substrates and how they change the design
- **Kraft / brown board:** colours go darker and warmer, and white needs a white ink layer. Show the client a kraft-simulated proof.
- **Clear film:** often **reverse printed** (artwork mirrored, printed on the inside), with a **white ink underlay** wherever colour must be opaque; leave white out where the product or metal should show through [SRC].
- **Metallised film:** colours can shimmer where there's no white underneath, which is a design tool (a "metallic" effect without foil).
- **Uncoated paper labels:** a softer, more crafted feel; colours print duller and dot gain is higher.
- **Coated board:** crisp, saturated colour.

## Sustainability (ask, and claim nothing unproven)
- Prefer mono-material structures where possible (easier to recycle), right-sized packs, fewer layers and less ink coverage.
- Any environmental claim ("recyclable", "compostable") must be true for the actual structure and market. The client supplies the evidence.
- Design for disassembly: labels that come off, separable windows.

## Structural ideas (where the budget allows)
A distinctive shape is one of the strongest brand assets, since product form leads recognition in some research [SRC]. Ideas include: an unusual opening, a window shaped like the brand's atom, a pack that becomes something after use (a holder, a planter, a game), and a shelf-ready tray that builds a brand wall.

## Food contact (food clients)
- Low-migration inks, or a functional barrier layer between ink and food, are standard practice for food contact. Ask the converter to confirm the ink system and laminate, and request their compliance documents [SRC].
- Print on the outside surface, or use reverse print inside a laminate, as the converter advises.

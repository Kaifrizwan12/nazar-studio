# Research behind nazar-story (2026-11-21)

Four passes: story structure, award and gallery presentation, attention on a scrolling page, and Behance specs. Tags follow `../../nazar-core/references/research_path.md`. Studios and brands studied are described as patterns only; none are named or copied here.

## 1. Story structure

**Screenwriting beats [SRC].** The 15-beat sheet from screenwriting runs Opening Image → Setup → Catalyst → Debate → Break into Two → Fun and Games → Midpoint → Bad Guys Close In → All Is Lost → Finale → Final Image. Two beats matter most for a case study: the **opening image** sets the tone in the first frame, and the **final image mirrors the opening**, proof that something changed. The guides call it a checklist of moments, not a template.
→ Rule: the last product panel echoes the first (same pack, new context or scale). The hook is a picture, not a title card.

**Editorial pacing [SRC].** Magazine pacing guides agree on four points.
- Alternate dense pages with open, image-led spreads.
- Use white space as a pause.
- Keep a few constants (running heads, a type system) for a sense of place, and vary scale, imagery and density for the emotional journey.
- Open with a "curtain raiser", often one dominant full-bleed image.

Editors plan the whole issue on a flatplan, where they can see when two features collide.
→ Rule: plan on a flatplan (the contact sheet) before building. Never put two dense pages side by side. Space out the big visual moments.

**Case-study writing [SRC].** Portfolio guides converge on problem → insight → solution → results, with the insight as its own beat and the research shown before the solution, so the logic reads in order. "Not a journal paper": keep it short.
→ Rule: the insight gets its own page and its own sentence. Copy stays under about 25 words a page (the kaif.md rule).

## 2. Award and gallery presentation

**Gallery curation [SRC].**
- Curators review a project once, at first publish, so publish it finished.
- Projects with fewer than 3 images are rarely featured.
- Upload about 1400 px wide, at consistent widths.
- The cover must appeal and also represent the project, and the full-size cover should repeat inside.
- Credit tools and collaborators.
- Quality, originality, presentation and context are weighed together.
- One curator reports about 5 seconds per project.
→ Rules: the cover is a product image that reads at thumbnail size, with no text. Panel 1 is the full-size hero. Every panel has the same width. Credits go at the end.

**Award entries [SRC].**
- Packaging awards ask for JPEG RGB images and a written description in round one. Physical samples and high-res files come only at shortlist.
- Student-award guidance caps the main presentation at 8 JPEG slides or a 2-minute film, and says to keep text inside images to a minimum. Other award boards cap board text at about 100 words, with no agency branding on the board.
- Juries' recurring pattern: the product shown plainly and truthfully (front, back, structure, detail), then in context, with very little text on the image.
→ Rules: show the real product, every side that matters, and keep the argument in short lines. An awards cut of the same story is 8 panels or fewer.

**What winning case studies do [IDEA, from studying featured packaging projects and studio case pages].**
- One strong opening image, usually the hero pack, very close or on its own colour.
- The idea stated in a line before any system pages.
- The system shown as rules, not as decoration.
- The range shown together once.
- Each format then gets one page with its own composition.
- Close-ups of finish and material.
- A real-world or "in hand" moment.
- A calm end on the studio and credits.
- Weak ones repeat one template for every panel, mock packs in cartoon scenes, or bury the product under callouts and arrows.

## 3. Attention on a scrolling page

- **Eyetracking, 2018 [SRC].** 120 users on 1920 × 1080 screens. 57% of viewing time went above the fold, about 17% to the second screenful, 74% to the first two and 81% to the first three. 65%+ of above-the-fold time fell in the top half of the viewport. The guidance: put the important things first, use a clear hierarchy, avoid "false floors" that make a page look finished.
- **Feed speed [SRC, secondary].** A platform-commissioned test found about 1.7 s per item on mobile (2.5 s on desktop), and recall after 0.25 s of exposure. Treat the number as indicative: it is widely repeated and the primary study is not public.
- **F-pattern [SRC].** On poorly structured pages readers scan in an F. Strong hierarchy breaks the pattern.

→ Rules:
- The first two panels carry the whole pitch: the hero and the idea.
- Every panel must read in about 2 s at phone width: one image, one line.
- Never end a panel on a flat empty band (a false floor). Let the next panel's colour or image start the change.
- Keep the core story to 12–20 panels. Attention past the third screen is a long tail, so put the payoff before it, not only at the end.

## 4. Behance specs [SRC, official help pages unless noted]

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Cover | Shown at 202 × 158. Upload at least 808 × 632; 1616 × 1264 is the 2x. No GIF covers. Cropped in Behance's tool, so keep the subject central |
| Project images | Shown 1400 px wide in project view, up to 2800 px in lightbox. Smaller images keep their size with side padding |
| Formats | JPEG (recommended), PNG, GIF (project content only) |
| File size | 10 MB or less recommended; over 50 MB rejected |
| Web uploader | JPEG/PNG. GIF and direct video go through the mobile apps (help page, 2026) |
| Video | Direct uploads show at most 1280 px wide (community-quoted). Embed from a video host for a full-width film |
| Count | No hard cap found. Fewer than 3 images is rarely featured |

The engine writes panels at 2880 × 1620 (2x of the 1440 grid; Behance scales to 1400/2800), PNG, numbered, with no folios, plus covers at 1616 × 1264, 808 × 632 and 768 × 576 (4:3).

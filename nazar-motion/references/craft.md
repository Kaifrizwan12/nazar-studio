# Craft

## 1. Animation principles (Disney's 12, applied to brand motion) [SRC]
Squash and stretch · anticipation · staging · straight-ahead vs pose-to-pose · follow-through and overlapping action · slow in and slow out (easing) · arcs · secondary action · timing · exaggeration · solid drawing (volume and consistency) · appeal. For sober brands, mostly use timing, easing, staging and follow-through. Squash, stretch and exaggeration are for playful brands only.

## 2. Logo animation patterns (choose from the idea, not the menu)
| Pattern | Works when |
|---|---|
| **Build** (the mark assembles from its parts) | The mark has meaningful parts (the atom, letters) |
| **Draw-on** (strokes trace the mark) | Line-based marks, craft brands |
| **Reveal** (a mask, wipe or light sweep) | Solid marks, premium tone |
| **Transform** (the atom morphs into the mark) | A strong small symbol |
| **Behaviour** (the mark does what the brand does: opens, stamps, syncs, pulses) | Always the best if the idea allows |
| **Typographic** (letters snap into kerning, weight changes) | Wordmarks |
| **Loop** (a subtle idle state) | Websites, screens, stickers |
Make versions: a full ident (1–2 s, with sound), a short sting (0.5–1 s), a loop, and a static end frame.

## 3. Storyboards
- 6–12 frames per piece, showing key poses, with timing notes (in seconds) and easing notes under each frame.
- Render the frames as stills with the core engine (exact logo, brand type), so the storyboard is on-brand.
- Approve the storyboard before animating. Changes are cheap on paper and expensive in After Effects.

## 4. Sound
- A **sonic logo** (0.5–2 s) is a distinctive asset like a logo. It should be simple, recognisable, and tied to the motion's key beat.
- Sound design follows the same personality as the motion (crisp, warm, analogue, digital).
- Use only licensed or original music; keep the licence records.
- Every piece must also work muted (captions, visual beats).

## 5. Cinematic devices (Kaif's dial, where the brand fits)
Title cards and end-credits pages · letterbox (2.39:1) framing inside 9:16 or 16:9 · scene and take numbering · film grain and halation (subtle) · "now showing" or trailer pacing (cold open → title → beats → end card) · the light of a scene as a palette (dusk, neon, tungsten) · match cuts on the brand's atom · slow push-ins on details. Use the grammar of film, never copyrighted footage or protected title designs.

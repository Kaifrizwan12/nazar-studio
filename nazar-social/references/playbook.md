# The team playbook (the deliverable) and QA

## Playbook contents (8–15 pages)
1. Our account in one line (the bio and the idea)
2. Profile: avatar, bio, highlights, pinned posts
3. Pillars with example posts
4. Templates: how to use each, with do and don't
5. Reels: our structures, captions, end card
6. Voice on social: hooks, captions, replies, local language, emoji rule
7. Hashtags and tagging
8. The launch grid (the first 9)
9. Calendar and rhythm
10. Community rules: replying, handling complaints, what we never engage with, escalation
11. What we measure: saves, shares, DMs, comments, profile visits, follows from posts (not just likes and views)
12. **The slop test**

## The slop test (every post must pass all 5)
1. Did a person make it, from real things (people, places, products)?
2. Is it specific: the place named, the detail real?
3. Does it carry the brand's idea, not just its logo?
4. Would it still be recognisably ours without the logo?
5. Would you stop scrolling for it, at thumbnail size?

## Social QA (on top of core `qa_design.md`)
- [ ] Every template is checked at phone size, in the 3:4 grid crop and in the 9:16 safe zone (using the app preview)
- [ ] Text contrast passes; captions burned into videos; alt text written
- [ ] The grid preview of the next 9–12 posts looks intentional
- [ ] Music and footage are licensed; people featured have agreed (releases kept)
- [ ] No protected characters, logos or footage used in pop-culture references (grammar only)
- [ ] Hooks are specific; captions pass the "could any brand post this?" test
- [ ] Templates are editable by the team in their tool, with locked brand elements
- [ ] Assets are named and organised in `assets/social/` with a README

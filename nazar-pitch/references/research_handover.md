# Research: handover and delivery packaging

All sources accessed **2026-11-21**. The tags follow `../../nazar-core/references/research_path.md`: [SRC] is a primary or official source, [KNOWN] is common practice reported by several secondary sources, [IDEA] is our own rule built from them. Platform specs change, so check them again before a launch. `engine/handover.py` keeps these numbers in its `PLATFORMS` table.

## 1. Design handover practice
- [SRC] The AIGA Standard Form of Agreement separates **Final Deliverables** ("the final versions of Deliverables provided by Designer and accepted by Client") from **Working Files** (the underlying files used to make the work). Whether working files are handed over is a contract decision, and the definition does not settle it. Usage rights in third-party elements are licensed by the client from the source. https://aiga.org/resources/aiga-standard-form-of-agreement-for-design-services · copy of the form: https://davidberman.com/wp-content/uploads/AIGA-Standard-Form-of-Agreement-for-Design-Services.pdf
- [KNOWN] Agency and designer checklists agree on the core pack: an editable master, vector print files (CMYK), vector and raster screen files (RGB: SVG, transparent PNG, JPG), files split by print and digital, a **predictable numbered folder structure with a readme, licensing and ownership notes**, and names that make sense to someone who did not make the files (no "final_final2"). Before sending, open the files on another device and check edges, transparency, colour and dimensions. https://support.99designs.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028764992-What-final-files-should-I-deliver-to-clients · https://yenra.com/logo-design/logo-handoff-checklist.txt · https://logosbynick.com/logo-files-for-clients/comment-page-2 · https://justcreative.com/2020/03/23/logo-design-file-formats-guide/ · https://artversion.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Brand-Identity-Design-Checklist-ArtVersion.pdf
- [KNOWN] No single standard handover template exists. Treat the format lists above as conventions, and the agreement decides what is in scope.

## 2. File names and paths
- [SRC] Windows reserved characters are `< > : " / \ | ? *`, NUL and the control characters 1 to 31. The reserved names are CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM1 to COM9, LPT1 to LPT9 and **COM¹ COM² COM³ LPT¹ LPT² LPT³** (superscript digits count), and they stay reserved with an extension (`NUL.txt`). A name must not end in a space or a period. Do not assume case sensitivity. Without the long-path opt-in, MAX_PATH is 260 characters, and File Explorer allows 255 per component. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/naming-a-file · https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/fileio/maximum-file-path-limitation
- [SRC] Office desktop apps are limited to about 256 characters for a full path, and OneDrive and SharePoint have their own limits. https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/onedrive/what-are-file-path-length-limits
- [KNOWN] macOS: HFS+ allows 255 UTF-16 units per name, normalised to NFD. APFS keeps the name exactly as written (it preserves normalisation and is insensitive to it), and macOS enforces about 255 in practice. A colon shows as a slash in Finder (it was the old separator). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HFS_Plus · https://eclecticlight.co/2024/03/25/apfs-directories-and-names/ · https://developer.apple.com/library/prerelease/content/documentation/FileManagement/Conceptual/APFS_Guide/FAQ/FAQ.html
- [SRC] ZIP: general-purpose **bit 11 (EFS)** set means the name and comment are UTF-8. Unset, the name is read as CP437, which is how accented names become garbage on old unzippers. Python's `zipfile` sets bit 11 automatically for non-ASCII names, and the self-test asserts it. PKWARE APPNOTE, Appendix D: https://flutter.googlesource.com/third_party/libzip/+/ae9c0c4f74cc4ac1a9dfc13c99a2e2811b885ae0/docs/appnote.txt · https://bugs.python.org/msg394505
- [KNOWN] Research-data naming guides: ISO 8601 dates (YYYY-MM-DD, numbers only), leading zeros on sequence numbers, the important part first, no spaces or special characters in machine names (use `-` or `_`), a period only before the extension, and the convention written in a README. https://datamanagement.hms.harvard.edu/collect/file-naming-conventions · https://instr.iastate.libguides.com/file-mgmt/naming · https://library.bu.edu/data_management/naming · https://researchdata.library.ubc.ca/plan/organize-your-data/
- [IDEA] Two naming modes. **human** for client folders: spaces and accents kept (NFC), and the numbers sort it ("02 Fruit pouch 100 g - Mango front.png"), because clients open these by hand. **safe** for portfolio, press and social: ASCII `A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ -` only, because uploaders, printers' FTPs and RIPs choke on anything else. Keep the relative path within 200 characters, so it still fits MAX_PATH after unzipping into a deep Downloads folder.

## 3. Portfolio platforms
- [SRC] **Behance**: the cover displays at 202×158 and must be at least **808×632** (crop tool after upload); 1616×1264 is the 2× of that. Project images: **JPEG/PNG 2800 px wide or smaller, 10 MB or less** (JPEG recommended), and images over 50 MB are not accepted. They display at 1400 px in the project view and up to 2800 px in the lightbox. GIF is allowed in the project but not as a cover, and GIFs must be looped before upload. Video: embedding from YouTube or Vimeo is recommended for a better experience. Shared-link thumbnails use the largest, most square static image near the top. https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483484-Guide-Project-Cover-Image · https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204484614 · https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483574-Guide-Uploading-Audio-Video-Files · https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/115004491127-FAQ-How-do-I-set-my-GIF-to-play-on-loop · https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/360034073754-Guide-Social-Media-Thumbnails (direct fetch returned 403. Figures are from the official pages' indexed text.)
- [SRC] **Dribbble**: images, GIFs and video at least **1600 px wide**, with no fixed ratio. Images (PNG/JPG/GIF) up to **10 MB**, video (MP4/MOV) up to **20 MB**. The thumbnail is cropped from any block. 1600×1200 (4:3) is the usual shot and thumbnail shape [KNOWN]. https://help.dribbble.com/en/articles/11062601-creating-dribbble-shots · https://dribbble.com/stories/2018/08/22/more-pixels-and-a-perfect-crop
- [KNOWN] Own site: 1200×630 is the common Open Graph share-image size.
- [IDEA] **Conflict found:** `nazar-story` renders panels at **2880** px wide, but Behance's own page says 2800 or smaller. The packager warns on every panel over 2800. Decide whether to render at 2800 (see LESSONS).

## 4. Print handover
- [SRC] PDF/X-4 (ISO 15930-7) keeps live transparency and layers, requires every font embedded (full or a complete subset), and requires an output intent with an **embedded** ICC profile (PDF/X-4p may reference an external one). Ghent Workgroup specs build on PDF/X-4. https://www.pdfa.org/pdfx-the-key-facts/ · https://img.ly/blog/what-does-print-ready-pdf-mean-understanding-pdf-x-standards-for-professional-printing/ · https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/When-PDF-is-production-critical.pdf
- [KNOWN] Printer checklist: bleed (commonly 3 mm, or 0.125 in, but the **printer's spec governs**), trim and bleed boxes set, fonts embedded or outlined (no "Not Embedded" in the font list), images at output resolution, colour in the printer's profile, a signed contract proof. Who is responsible for press-ready files is a contract matter. https://www.fespa.com/en/club-fespa-online/how-to-guides/press-ready-files-–-who-is-responsible · https://help.lob.com/print-and-mail/designing-mail-creatives/creative-formatting/pdf-preflight-checklist
- [IDEA] The packager reads each PDF's `GTS_PDFXVersion` and prints it in the README. In a press target it warns on any PDF that is not PDF/X. It does not prove fonts are embedded: that check stays in the preflight tool (`nazar-packaging`).

## 5. Licences
- [SRC] SIL OFL FAQ (1.1-update7): artwork made with an OFL font is **not** under the OFL (1.13), and fonts may be embedded in PDFs in full or as a subset (1.12). Logos and any design work need no extra permission (1.1). If the font files are handed over, they go with their copyright notice and licence (1.7), and they are never sold on their own. Reserved Font Names may not be used for modified versions. https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/ · https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-reserved-font-names/
- [KNOWN] Commercial fonts are licensed per user or seat, and usually cannot be passed on. The client buys their own licence, and the handover lists who holds each licence (font, stock, music).

## 6. AI disclosure
- [SRC] EU AI Act Art. 50: the deployer duties to disclose deepfakes have applied since **2 August 2026**. Commission guidelines were published 20 July 2026, and there is a voluntary Code of Practice. The guidelines' own example: **a real product against an AI-generated background is not a deepfake** if it does not mislead about the product, but an AI image that misrepresents the product is one. For evidently artistic work the duty is lighter. Fines run up to EUR 15 M or 3% of turnover. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content · https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/ · https://www.orrick.com/en/insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50 · https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august
- [SRC] Adobe attaches C2PA Content Credentials automatically when its generative tools are used, but does not add a visible label. Behance shows Content Credentials (Inspect), and Fresco can attach a "Created without generative AI" label. C2PA has added a `c2pa.ai-disclosure` assertion. https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/apps/generative-ai/ai-content-labeling-faq.html · https://helpx.adobe.com/fresco/using/content-credentials-fresco.html · https://c2pa.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2026/07/Use-of-Content-Credentials-to-Identify-Synthetic-and-Non-Synthetic-Content.pdf
- [IDEA] Our hybrid rule (AI plate, real product render) sits on the safe side of the guidelines, but we still disclose: one README line and an `[AI plate]` tag on each affected file. Content Credentials already in the files are kept, never stripped. Not found: whether Behance has its own project-level AI field. Check in the uploader.

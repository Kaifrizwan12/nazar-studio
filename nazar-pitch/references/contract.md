# Agreement: the key terms

Base the agreement on a recognised template, such as the **AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services**, which is modular (terms and conditions plus the proposal form the agreement) and offers several intellectual-property ownership options [SRC]. Have a lawyer adapt it to local law. Not legal advice.

## Terms to include
| Term | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Scope and deliverables** | Exactly what's included (from the proposal), and what's excluded |
| **Fees and payment** | Amounts, schedule, deposit, late fees, currency, taxes |
| **Expenses** | Printing, licences, travel, photography: billed at cost or with a handling fee, with prior approval |
| **Revisions** | A fixed number of rounds per phase; extra rounds billed hourly or via a new change request [SRC] |
| **Change requests** | How out-of-scope work is requested, quoted and approved before it starts [SRC] |
| **Timeline and client delays** | The schedule depends on timely client feedback; delays move dates and may incur fees |
| **Approvals** | The named decision-maker; written approval (email counts) at each phase |
| **Intellectual property** | Ownership of final approved deliverables transfers on **full payment**. Preliminary concepts and unused routes remain the designer's. Work-for-hire usually doesn't apply automatically, so use an express assignment [SRC] |
| **Third-party licences** | Fonts, images, music and code are licensed, not owned. List them, and say who pays for them |
| **Portfolio rights** | The designer may show the work (after launch, or with any confidentiality respected) |
| **Confidentiality** | Both sides keep confidential information private |
| **Cancellation / kill fee** | If the client cancels, they pay for work done to date plus a kill fee (often a percentage of the remaining fee); the rights to unfinished work stay with the designer [SRC] |
| **Warranties and liability** | The client warrants that the content they supply is theirs to use; the designer's liability is limited to the fees paid |
| **Trademark clearance** | The client is responsible for legal trademark clearance; the designer provides a first-pass search only |
| **Termination, dispute resolution, governing law** | How either side can end it, and where disputes are resolved |

# Positioning

## 1. The onlyness statement (Marty Neumeier, *Zag*)
Fill in all six parts [SRC]:
> Our **[what: category]** is the ONLY **[how: differentiator]**
> for **[who: customer]**
> in **[where: market]**
> who **[why: need state]**
> during **[when: underlying trend]**.
Test: if you can't write "the only", you don't have a position yet. If a competitor could sign it, rewrite it. Neumeier's later test: if you need more than ~12 words to say what's "only", it's too complicated.
Use it as a **filter**: every later decision (name, look, channel) is checked against it.

## 2. The tension
Strong brands sit on a productive contradiction. Write it as "X × Y" and explain why both halves are true.
Examples: craft × scale (a small roaster supplying chains), heritage × speed (an old bank that moves fast), local × global. The tension becomes the identity's engine (two textures, a two-part copy device, a two-colour system).

## 3. Competitor map
- Find 6–10 competitors (direct, indirect and "the alternative": doing nothing, making it at home).
- For each: their promise in their own words, their look (screenshot or file in `research/`), price tier, channel.
- Plot them on **two axes that matter to the buyer** (not to the designer), e.g. everyday ↔ special, functional ↔ indulgent, local ↔ imported.
- Find the empty quadrant. If it's empty for a reason (nobody wants it), say so.
- Also map the **visual codes** of the category: the colours, shapes and type everyone uses. These are the conventions to break or deliberately keep (`../nazar-core/references/masters.md`: distinctive, not just different).

## 4. Audience portraits (not personas with stock photos)
2–3 portraits, each written from research or real conversations: their day, where they meet the brand, what they believe now, what we want them to believe, and the words they actually use. No invented demographics dressed up as insight. Mark anything assumed.

## 5. Beliefs (3, at most 5)
Each one written as a stance someone could disagree with: "AI assists the hands, never the brain", not "We value quality". Each belief has a proof point ("we show this by …").

## 6. Brand idea (the one sentence)
Combine the onlyness statement and the tension into one memorable sentence the identity can hang on. Deutsche Bank's mark, for example, carries "growth in a stable environment" (a slash in a square). This sentence goes to `nazar-logo` and `nazar-brand-book`.

## 7. Strategy QA
- [ ] Every claim is backed by research or labelled an assumption
- [ ] The onlyness statement has all six parts, and no competitor could sign it
- [ ] The tension is real (both halves are true) and productive (it suggests visuals)
- [ ] The competitor map's axes matter to buyers
- [ ] Beliefs are disagreeable stances with proof points
- [ ] The brand idea is under ~10 words and can be pictured

# Brand architecture

## The four models (David Aaker's brand relationship spectrum) [SRC]
| Model | How it looks | Example | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branded house** | One master brand plus descriptors | Apple (iPhone, Watch), FedEx Express | Offers share an audience and promise; one story; efficient |
| **Sub-brand** | The master leads, and a sub-name adds personality | Master + "Gold" range | A range needs its own character but benefits from the master |
| **Endorsed** | A standalone name leads, with "by Master" supporting | Courtyard by Marriott | Different audiences or price points; acquisitions; some protection |
| **House of brands** | Separate brands, parent invisible | P&G (Tide, Pampers) | Very different audiences or positions; crisis firewall |
Most companies end up with a **hybrid**. Decide the architecture as business strategy first, then design it.

## Decision tests
1. Do the offers share an audience and a promise? → leans towards a branded house
2. Would a crisis in one line damage the others? → separate (endorsed or house of brands)
3. Do they need very different positions or prices? → separate
4. Is the master brand an asset in this new market? → use it (branded house or endorsed)
5. Is there budget to build more than one brand? If not → branded house
6. Acquisition? → usually endorse first, migrate later

## Deliverables
- An architecture diagram (a tree), with the model named for each branch
- Naming rules: how new products are named (descriptor only? a sub-name? a family pattern?)
- Lockup rules: master + descriptor, master + sub-brand, endorsed lockup (size ratio, position)
- A visual relationship rule: what's shared (colour, type, atom) and what flexes (accent, pattern) per range
- For packaging ranges, hand this to `nazar-packaging` (range architecture)

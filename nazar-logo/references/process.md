# Logo process

## 1. The one-page brief
The brand idea and onlyness statement (from strategy), audience, where the mark lives, scripts, must-haves, never-haves, competitors, and a **success test** in concrete terms (e.g. "recognisable as a 32 px avatar and on a vehicle at 20 m").

## 2. Research
- **Competitor marks wall:** every competitor and category mark on one sheet, same size, in black. Note the shared codes (everyone uses a leaf, a globe, a swoosh, a script). These are the conventions to break or knowingly keep.
- **Category archive:** old trade marks, labels, stamps and signage for the category (core `research_path.md`, pass 1). Look for a forgotten form worth reviving.
- **Mark archives** [KNOWN]: Logobook (a black-and-white mark archive); Per Mollerup, *Marks of Excellence* (a taxonomy of trademarks); Yasaburo Kuwayama, *Trademarks & Symbols of the World*; Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv, *Identify*; Unit Editions, *Manuals 1 & 2*; trademark registries' figurative searches; the Japanese *kamon* tradition (family crests: a strict system of simple, enclosed emblems), and merchant and house marks (some of the earliest trade marks).
- **The name:** its letters, sound, meaning and shape in each script. Write and draw it 50 times.
- **The place and culture:** local lettering, stamps, coins, signage, patterns, the colours of the street.

## 3. Concept session (see core `ideation.md`)
1. **Reframe** the problem 5 ways (Gill), and pick the most interesting.
2. Run **at least 3 generators**: word → image collisions (Games), the name holds the mark (look for hidden shapes and the negative space, as in the FedEx arrow), a semiotic choice (icon / index / symbol), random entry or provocation (de Bono), a morphological matrix, behaviour first.
3. Produce **quantity**: 50–100 ideas or thumbnails. No judging.
4. Cluster them into **3–5 territories**: name each one and give it a one-line meaning. Typical territories:
   - From the **name** (a letter, ligature, diacritic, counter or hidden shape)
   - From the **product or process** (form, cross-section, tool, trace)
   - From the **place or culture** (a motif, a lettering tradition, a map shape)
   - From the **tension** (two halves, before/after, inside/outside)
   - From a **behaviour** (it opens, fills, counts, travels, blinks)

## 4. Sketching
- Thumbnails at small size first, because that's how marks are mostly seen.
- 50–100+ → 15–20 better sketches → 6–9 digital candidates → **3 routes** (one sharp-and-safe, one bold, one unorthodox).
- Kill anything on the core kill list: clichés, ideas that need explaining, effects carrying the idea, lookalikes.
- Keep the sketch sheets. They're part of the story in the presentation and the brand book.

## 5. Development (per route)
Build in vector on a construction grid (`craft.md`). Develop the wordmark pairing, lockups and colour, and test small sizes early. **All routes reach the same level of finish**, so no route loses just for being rough.
Keep looking while refining: spacing, negative space and accidents can reveal the idea (the FedEx arrow appeared during letter-spacing work).

## Timeline guide (for proposals)
Brief and research 15% · concepts and sketching 25% · development 25% · refinement and tests 20% · files and handover 15%.

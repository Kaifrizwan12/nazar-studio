# Kinds of marks

| Type | What | Strengths | Risks | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark** | The name in custom or customised lettering | Says the name; strong for new brands | Long names; needs a separate icon for avatars | New brands, distinctive names |
| **Lettermark / monogram** | Initials or one letter | Compact; icon-ready | Generic; hard to own | Long names, established brands |
| **Abstract symbol** | A geometric form | Flexible across languages | Needs investment to gain meaning | Global, multi-language, tech |
| **Pictorial symbol** | A recognisable thing | Instantly understood | Literal; clichés; easy to imitate | Local, crafts, food (with care) |
| **Combination** | Symbol + wordmark | Versatile; can be split later | Two things to make great | Most new brands |
| **Emblem / badge** | Text inside a shape | Heritage, authority | Fails small; detail fills in | Heritage, institutions, labels (with a simplified small version) |
| **Character mark** | A mascot as the logo | Warm, memorable | Dates fast; hard to scale | Kids, mass market |
| **Dynamic / flexible** | Changes within rules (e.g. a mark that holds content) | Expressive; content-led | Needs a fixed core for recognition | Culture, media, platforms |
| **Bilingual lockup** | Two scripts as one mark | Local + global | Balance and alignment are hard | Multi-script markets |

## Choosing
- An unknown name → wordmark or combination (say the name until it's known).
- A long or hard name → a strong symbol or monogram plus the wordmark.
- Mostly seen tiny (app, social) → a symbol that holds at 16–32 px.
- Mostly seen big and far (outdoor, signage) → a bold silhouette and open counters.
- Plan the **small-size symbol** (the atom) from day one, not afterwards.
- Semiotics: decide whether it's an icon, an index or a symbol (core `ideation.md` D), and write down why.

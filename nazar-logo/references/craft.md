# Craft: construction and optical correction

## 1. Construction
- Build on a **grid** (a square module, circles of related radii, a fixed angle set such as 15°, 30° or 45°, or a golden or root-2 ratio only if it truly helps). A grid makes the mark consistent and extendable (icons, patterns).
- Limit the vocabulary: one corner radius (or two related ones), one stroke weight family, one angle family.
- Draw with the **fewest anchor points** possible. Put points on extrema (top, bottom, left, right of curves), keep handles horizontal or vertical where possible, and make curves smooth (no kinks where curves meet).
- Keep the **construction drawing** (grid + circles + key measures). It goes into the brand book and proves the logic.
- Then correct by eye. Aicher's finished Munich pictograms departed from his own generative grid, and that's normal [SRC].

## 2. Optical corrections (the difference between amateur and pro)
| Issue | Correction |
|---|---|
| Round and pointed forms look smaller than flat ones | **Overshoot**: let circles and points extend slightly past the baseline and cap height (often about 1–3% of the height) |
| Horizontal strokes look heavier than vertical ones of the same width | Make horizontals slightly thinner |
| Joins and crossings fill in (darken) at small sizes | **Ink traps** or thinning where strokes meet |
| A geometric circle looks flat at its sides | Slightly "super-elliptical" adjustments |
| A mathematically centred shape looks off-centre | Centre by **visual mass**, not the bounding box (triangles, play icons, asymmetric shapes) |
| Diagonals look thinner than verticals | Slightly increase diagonal stroke weight |
| White on dark looks bolder than dark on white | Make a **reversed version** slightly lighter if needed |
| The symbol and wordmark look unbalanced | Balance by optical weight and size, not equal heights |

## 3. Wordmark craft
- Start from type you understand, then customise: adjust weight, terminals, apertures, x-height and widths. Fully custom lettering is drawn from sketches.
- **Kern by eye, at display size and at small size.** Default metrics fail on custom forms. Problem pairs: AV, AT, LT, Ty, To, Yo, rn, and any round next to straight. Tools' "optical" kerning is a start, not an end.
- Even colour (texture): squint, and no letter should look darker or lighter than the rest.
- **Open counters and apertures** for small sizes; tight forms fill in on screens and in print.
- Consider a **small-size (micro) version** with heavier strokes, wider spacing and simplified details, as type foundries do with optical sizes.
- Never stretch or squash type to fit. Never fake bold or italic.
- Check the licence of any typeface used as a base. A customised commercial font usually still needs a licence for logo use.

## 4. Bilingual lockups
- Each script drawn natively. Don't force one script's forms onto the other.
- Match the **visual size and weight**, not the point size.
- Choose a relationship: side by side (shared baseline or centre line), stacked (shared width or centre axis), or interlocked (shared stroke or element: the hardest and the strongest).
- RTL and LTR reading order: decide which reads first in which market, and supply both lockup orders if needed.
- Shared elements (a dot, a stroke, an angle) make the two read as one family.

## 5. Colour
- Design in **black first**. The idea must work in one colour.
- Then colour: one primary brand colour (an ownable distinctive asset), with tested contrast on light and dark.
- Gradients, foils and effects are optional extras, never required for recognition. Always supply a flat version.

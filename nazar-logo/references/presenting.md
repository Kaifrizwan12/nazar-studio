# Presenting routes

## Before the meeting
- **2–3 routes**, all at the same finish. Never 10 (it says you couldn't decide).
- A recommendation, and why.
- Rehearse the story of each route in **one sentence**, then 3 lines.

## Order of the presentation
1. **Recap:** the brief, the brand idea, the success test (2 pages). Agree on these before showing any marks.
2. **What we found:** the competitor wall and the cliché to avoid (1–2 pages).
3. **For each route:**
   - Its one-sentence story
   - The mark alone, large, on white
   - The construction or the idea revealed (a sketch, the hidden shape)
   - Small-size and one-colour proof
   - 4–6 real applications (avatar, sign, pack, screen, merch), drawn as clean flat applications, not cartoon mockups
   - The test sheet
4. **Recommendation:** which route and why, tied back to the brief.
5. **Next steps:** what happens after a choice, and what's fixed versus still open.

## Presenting well (Mike Monteiro and others)
- Lead with the problem and the thinking, then the work. Presenting without the reasoning is a professional failure.
- Every element shown is there for a reason. Don't give a "real estate tour" of everything on the screen [SRC].
- The client hired an expert. Recommend; don't just ask what they like.
- Let the work be seen in silence for a moment before talking.
- Show marks in context early, since nobody experiences a logo floating on white.

## Handling feedback
- Ask **problem questions**, not taste questions: "Which route best delivers [the brand idea] to [the audience]?" rather than "Which do you like?"
- Turn opinions into problems: "I don't like green" → "What is the green failing to do?"
- Note all feedback, then answer it in writing with what will change and what won't (and why).
- Don't combine routes on request ("Frankenlogos"). Explain why each route works as a whole. Offer to push the strongest one further instead.
- Agree a decision-maker and a deadline. Committee feedback without a decision-maker stalls everything.
- Revision rounds are limited by contract (`nazar-pitch`). Extra rounds are a change request.

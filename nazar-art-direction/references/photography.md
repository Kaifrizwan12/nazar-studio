# Photography direction

## 1. References that work
- Collect 20–40 references from photographers, film stills, archives and the place itself. For each, **name the quality**: light direction, colour grade, crop, depth of field, composition, styling, mood [SRC].
- Cinematic references (Kaif's taste) are a strong source: a film's palette, the time of day, the lens feel. Cite the film and scene as a reference; never use stills in deliverables.

## 2. Style rules (one page per image type)
| Rule | Decide |
|---|---|
| **Point of view** | One line ("close, warm, mid-action") |
| **Light** | Lighting direction is a major driver of mood [SRC]: natural / window / golden hour / hard flash / tungsten / neon; direction and contrast |
| **Colour grade** | Warm/cool, saturation, contrast, how skin tones look, how brand colours appear; a LUT or preset with before/after examples [SRC] |
| **Composition** | Framing, negative space for type, horizon, symmetry or not, letterbox for cinematic sets |
| **Lens and depth** | Wide and close vs long and compressed; shallow or deep focus |
| **People** | Real vs cast; how they look at the camera (or don't); diversity; hands and gestures |
| **Styling and props** | Brand colours appear naturally; no clutter; real objects |
| **Crop ratios** | 4:5 and 1:1 (social), 9:16 (stories), 16:9 (web, banners), print formats; shoot both orientations [SRC] |
| **Never** | 3–5 things this brand never shows (e.g. fake smiles, generic handshakes, white-background clichés) |

## 3. Shoot brief (send to the photographer)
Brand idea in one line · purpose and uses · point of view · references with named qualities · style rules · the shot list · people and casting · locations · wardrobe, props, styling · schedule · deliverables (number of finals, formats, ratios, resolution, retouching level) · usage rights · budget · contacts.

## 4. Shot list and call sheet
- **Shot list:** shot number · description · purpose and placement (e.g. "web hero, needs space on the left for headline") · ratio and orientation · people and props · priority (must / nice). Keep it realistic for the time available [SRC].
- **Call sheet:** date, call times, location addresses and access, crew and contacts, weather plan, schedule by shot, safety notes.

## 5. On set
- Shoot the must-haves first, then the variations.
- Check frames against the style rules and the layout needs (space for type, safe zones).
- Capture extra: details, textures, behind the scenes (for social), and video snippets.
- Model and property releases signed on the day.

## 6. Grading and retouching
- Grade consistently from a master preset or LUT; match a reference frame.
- Retouching level agreed upfront: clean-up (dust, stray hairs, distractions) vs heavy (compositing). Don't alter bodies or product truth beyond what's honest.
- Deliver masters (high-res, colour-managed) plus web versions. Name them `<brand>_<shoot>_<subject>_<nn>.<ext>`.

## 7. Rights
Record for every image: the photographer, the licence (where, how long, which media), model releases, property releases, and any restrictions. Keep this in `assets/imagery/RIGHTS.md`.

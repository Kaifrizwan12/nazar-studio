# Photography direction

## 1. References that work
- Collect 20–40 references from photographers, film stills, archives and the place itself. For each, **name the quality**: light direction, colour grade, crop, depth of field, composition, styling, mood [SRC].
- Cinematic references (Kaif's taste) are a strong source: a film's palette, the time of day, the lens feel. Cite the film and scene as a reference; never use stills in deliverables.

## 1b. Non-orthodox effects, named and real
When the brief wants to break "default good taste" on purpose, reach for a real documented technique with a real origin, not a filter name. Each of these is a deliberate creative choice a known photographer, designer or brand made and that became iconic because of it — not a generic preset.

| Effect | Known for | How it's actually done | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bleach bypass (skip bleach) | Invented by Kazuo Miyagawa for Kon Ichikawa's *Her Brother* (1960); Janusz Kamiński's high-ENR (partial bleach bypass) process on *Saving Private Ryan* for its harsh, desaturated look | Partially or fully skip the bleach step in C-41 colour film processing, leaving silver in the print: deep blacks, crushed highlights, a metallic cast. Digitally: desaturate and raise contrast while adding density in the shadows — not just a flat saturation pull | Grim, weighty, "something's wrong here" editorial; conflict, industrial or stripped-back narratives |
| X-ray photography | Nick Veasey, who built the world's first mobile X-ray studio; an ongoing V&A fashion-collection X-ray project since 2016 | Industrial X-ray equipment, long exposures on film or digital sensor, often composited from several exposures of a disassembled subject | Revealing "what's really inside" a product, material honesty, pack-contents imagery |
| Skull/skeleton motif as a running signature | Alexander McQueen — a skeleton seated in the audience and bone headpieces for *Dante* A/W1996, the Skull Scarf's debut at *Irère* S/S2003 — stated inspiration from Andreas Vesalius's anatomical drawings | One motif (skull, spine, bone) repeated across print, jewellery and silhouette through an entire collection, tied to a stated idea (here: memento mori), not used as one-off decoration | Luxury with a dark edge; mortality or transience as a brand idea |
| Scanography (scanner-as-camera) | Joseph Scheer's scanned-moth work (Alfred University); the "Scanner as Camera" group show, Washington and Lee University, 2008 | The object sits directly on a flatbed scanner's glass; the scanner's own sweeping light source makes the image — extreme macro detail, flattened depth, a black background by default | Botanical or product detail shots, deliberately flat and hyper-crisp instead of "photographic" |
| In-camera double exposure | William Klein's Vogue fashion work, where a second few-second exposure let an assistant draw light shapes around the model with a flashlight | Two exposures on one frame, done live in-camera, not composited afterward | Movement and energy; fashion as performance rather than fashion as product |
| Direct on-camera flash, "snapshot aesthetic" | Rooted in art photography (Nan Goldin, Wolfgang Tillmans — diaristic, unglamorous); Juergen Teller is the cleanest active commercial reference | A plain background (often a white wall), bare on-camera flash with no diffusion: flat harsh light, a visible shadow edge, deliberately "undone" | Anti-glossy, youth or streetwear work; "this is real, not styled" |
| True thermal (heat-camera) imaging | The Royals' A/W2018 campaign for Sydney's Strand Arcade, shot in the dark on an actual thermal/FLIR camera to argue touch matters more than looks when buying clothes | A genuine thermal camera, not an infrared colour filter — most "infrared" editorial is a filter effect, not real heat imaging | A once-per-campaign stunt idea about materiality and touch, not a repeatable system |

Two honesty notes: the exact process behind *Seven*'s famously bleak grade is disputed between full bleach bypass and a different silver-retention print process — don't over-claim which. And the direct-flash look's most commonly cited name, Terry Richardson, carries documented sexual-misconduct allegations since 2001; cite Teller, Goldin and Tillmans as the lineage instead unless a client specifically needs the fuller history.

## 2. Style rules (one page per image type)
| Rule | Decide |
|---|---|
| **Point of view** | One line ("close, warm, mid-action") |
| **Light** | Lighting direction is a major driver of mood [SRC]: natural / window / golden hour / hard flash / tungsten / neon; direction and contrast |
| **Colour grade** | Warm/cool, saturation, contrast, how skin tones look, how brand colours appear; a LUT or preset with before/after examples [SRC] |
| **Composition** | Framing, negative space for type, horizon, symmetry or not, letterbox for cinematic sets |
| **Lens and depth** | Wide and close vs long and compressed; shallow or deep focus |
| **People** | Real vs cast; how they look at the camera (or don't); diversity; hands and gestures |
| **Styling and props** | Brand colours appear naturally; no clutter; real objects |
| **Crop ratios** | 4:5 and 1:1 (social), 9:16 (stories), 16:9 (web, banners), print formats; shoot both orientations [SRC] |
| **Never** | 3–5 things this brand never shows (e.g. fake smiles, generic handshakes, white-background clichés) |

## 3. Shoot brief (send to the photographer)
Brand idea in one line · purpose and uses · point of view · references with named qualities · style rules · the shot list · people and casting · locations · wardrobe, props, styling · schedule · deliverables (number of finals, formats, ratios, resolution, retouching level) · usage rights · budget · contacts.

## 4. Shot list and call sheet
- **Shot list:** shot number · description · purpose and placement (e.g. "web hero, needs space on the left for headline") · ratio and orientation · people and props · priority (must / nice). Keep it realistic for the time available [SRC].
- **Call sheet:** date, call times, location addresses and access, crew and contacts, weather plan, schedule by shot, safety notes.

## 5. On set
- Shoot the must-haves first, then the variations.
- Check frames against the style rules and the layout needs (space for type, safe zones).
- Capture extra: details, textures, behind the scenes (for social), and video snippets.
- Model and property releases signed on the day.

## 6. Grading and retouching
- Grade consistently from a master preset or LUT; match a reference frame.
- Retouching level agreed upfront: clean-up (dust, stray hairs, distractions) vs heavy (compositing). Don't alter bodies or product truth beyond what's honest.
- Deliver masters (high-res, colour-managed) plus web versions. Name them `<brand>_<shoot>_<subject>_<nn>.<ext>`.

## 7. Rights
Record for every image: the photographer, the licence (where, how long, which media), model releases, property releases, and any restrictions. Keep this in `assets/imagery/RIGHTS.md`.

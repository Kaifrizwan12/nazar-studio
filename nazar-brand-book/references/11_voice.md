# 11 · Voice and copy

## The voice page
- **Three traits**, each written as "X, not Y" (for example: warm, not sweet · sure, not loud · local, not folksy).
- **One copy device**: a repeatable shape for lines that carries the idea. For example: every line has two halves, one for each side of the brand's tension.
  Other devices: question → answer · before → after · a proverb rewritten · a number + a feeling · a label format ("Net wt. 200 g of patience") · topical puns (the Amul formula).
- **Ten sample lines**, then **three ads** that use them.
- **Do / don't words**: 5 words the brand uses and 5 it never uses.

## Writing rules for the whole book
- Most pages: one title sentence (under 10 words) plus one supporting line (under 25 words).
- Plain words. No marketing filler ("elevate", "seamless", "unleash", "curated", "journey").
- Sentence case for titles. Caps only in mono labels and the display tagline.
- Write in the brand's languages as equals. A transliterated local language is upright, with no italics or quotes. When it's needed, the meaning goes in the secondary colour on the next line.
- Numbers are real, or marked "sample".
- Every page's words must agree with the locked idea. If a line could belong to any brand, rewrite it.

## The tone dial (intake Q9)
Show one line in three voices so the client can point at one. For example, a coffee roaster:
- Sober: "Roasted on Tuesday. In your cup by Friday."
- Warm: "Roasted this week, for your slow mornings."
- Playful: "Fresher than your excuses for being late."

## The dictionary page (a favourite front-matter device)
Headword in the text face · pronunciation in mono (plain transliteration, because some mono fonts lack IPA glyphs) · part of speech · gloss · related entries as guide words. Use it for the name or the tagline.

# 10 · Applications

## The spec-sheet rule
Applications are **production spec sheets, not mockups**. Each sheet shows:
- The format drawn flat at true proportion (dieline, panel, screen), with the brand's artwork placed
- Dimension lines with real sizes in mono (mm for print, px for screen)
- A title block: format name, size, material or substrate, finish, colours (spot/CMYK), file name, page id
- Callouts with dotted leaders for the rules (logo position, safe area, bleed)
- Optionally, a small "twin" panel showing the same format's digital or social counterpart
No perspective mockups, no cartoon objects, no stock scenes, no AI renders. If he wants a realistic mockup for Behance, that's a separate deliverable made from a licensed mockup or a real photo.

If the client's brand appears on the agency's own formats, tag every sheet as "client format" or "own format", and use the brand mark as the stand-in for client artwork.

## Packaging specifics (food)
- Mandatory panels vary by market. For Pakistan, check PSQCA / Punjab Food Authority labelling (ingredients, net weight, manufacturer address, batch number, dates, a halal mark if certified). Ask the client for the exact legal text, and never invent certification marks.
- Show the dieline with glue flap, bleed (3 mm), safe area and fold lines.
- Substrate changes colour (see `05_colour.md`), so show the kraft version if kraft is used.
- Window cut-outs: the product *is* the graphic. Design around what shows through.

## The social playbook (section 07, when in scope)
Written for the team that will actually post:
1. **Profile**: handle, avatar (the atom, not the full lockup), bio (2 lines, both languages), highlights covers
2. **Pillars**: 3–4 content pillars with local-language names and a one-line rule each
3. **Templates**: 3 that cover almost everything (a line post, a photo post, a story), with safe areas
4. **Reels**: a 5-beat structure tied to the brand idea, cover rules, captions on screen
5. **Captions and tags**: length, language mix, hashtag rule (one owned tag plus 2–3 descriptive), no-emoji or emoji rule
6. **Launch plan**: the first 9 posts as a grid preview
7. **The slop test**: 5 questions every post must pass (did a person make it? is it real and named? does it carry the idea? would it work without the logo? would you stop scrolling?)

## Master practices for applications (see `../nazar-core/references/masters.md`)
- **The shelf test:** place the pack among 6–10 competitor packs at real shelf size, then view it at 2 m, in greyscale. The brand and the variant must read in under 2 seconds.
- **Brand block + variant cue:** a consistent brand block across the range, and one variant cue (colour or image) that never breaks the block.
- **Keep what's known:** on a refresh, keep the recognised assets (colour, shape, character). Change them only with evidence.
- **The product is the hero:** windows, real colour, real form.
- **Grid sheets per family** (Crouwel): each application family gets a grid template shown on its spec sheet.
- **Make your own:** finish the section with one format the client didn't ask for, built only from the rules, to prove the system works.

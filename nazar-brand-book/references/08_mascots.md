# 08 · Mascots and characters

## The test: does this brand need one?
Say yes only if at least two are true:
- The audience includes children, families or a mass market that buys on recognition (snacks, dairy, apps).
- The brand needs a voice that can comment on the news or the season (a topical character).
- The product is invisible or abstract (insurance, software, logistics) and needs a face.
- The brand will be licensed or merchandised.
Otherwise offer a **quiet character**: the atom with a personality (posture, a wink, a habit) but no face.

## What the great ones teach (research pass 8)
| Character | Lesson | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Michelin's Bibendum** (1898) | Built from the product itself (a stack of tyres). Simplified over a century while keeping its core visual DNA. | [SRC] Creative Review, Logo Design Love |
| **The Amul girl** (1966, daCunha) | A topical character: she comments on the week's news in a pun. One visual formula, endless lines. She sustained India's longest-running ad campaign. | [SRC] Amul, The Better India |
| **Kumamon** (Kumamoto, 2010) | Simple enough to draw in seconds. Free licensing traded fees for exposure, but within strict rules (a fixed set of official poses). | [SRC] Wikipedia, Monocle, dentsu |
| **Mailchimp's Freddie** | Personality rules on paper: he winks and smiles but never speaks, so copy is never written in his voice. | [SRC] Mailchimp style guide |
| **Duolingo's Duo** | A documented set of poses and emotions, and an always-in-character writing guide. The character can break the rules on social when it's deliberate. | [SRC] design.duolingo.com |
| **Regional and folk characters** | Regional characters carry national memory. Borrow the logic of folk motifs (repetition, symmetry, protection), never the exact motif. | [KNOWN] |

## If yes, the mascot spread (4–6 pages)
1. **Origin**: where the character comes from (from the product, the place or a pun). One line of meaning.
2. **Construction**: built from the atom and simple geometry, with the construction lines shown like a technical drawing.
3. **Turnaround**: front, ¾ and side (if it's 3D) or a single fixed view (like Freddie facing right).
4. **Expression sheet**: 6–8 approved states, each tied to a use (welcome, sold out, thanks, error, sale, festival).
5. **Voice rules**: does it speak? In which language? Three sample lines, and three things it never says or does.
6. **Misuse**: no recolouring, no stretching, no new poses without approval, no tragedy or politics.

## Rules
- Draw it as vector geometry in the engine, or from the illustrator's own files. **Never generate a mascot with AI.**
- Keep it simple: it must survive at 32 px and as a single-colour stamp.
- Give it a name in the local language, with a story of one sentence.
- Make sure it works in the brand's single-accent palette.

## The distinctive-asset test (Romaniuk)
A character only earns its place if it scores on **fame** (people come to recognise it) and **uniqueness** (it points to this brand alone). A generic smiling fruit fails uniqueness. Build it from the brand's own atom so it can't belong to anyone else.

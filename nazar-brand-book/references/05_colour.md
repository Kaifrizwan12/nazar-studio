# 05 · Colour: palettes from evidence

## Method (do it in this order)
1. **Harvest.** Pull 15–30 candidate colours from the research, each with a source: a place surface (a painted wall, a vehicle, a market awning), the product itself (dried apricot flesh, fig skin, almond skin), an archive plate (a Wada combination, a Werner swatch), a historic pigment (lapis, malachite, orpiment yellow, lac red, hingul/cinnabar), or a period print process (riso fluorescent pink, two-colour litho, newsprint grey). Sample the hex from a real scan whenever you can, and write down where it came from.
2. **Pick the roles.** A Kaif palette is usually **3 neutrals + 1 accent**, plus at most one special (a gradient, a foil or a second accent reserved for one job).
   - Base (≈60%), Light (≈30%), Secondary text, Accent (≈10%). This is Itten's *contrast of extension*: a bright colour needs far less area to balance a dull one.
   - The accent should come from the brand's own artwork or product, so it's owned rather than borrowed (for example, sampled from one stop of the logo's gradient).
3. **Name them in the brand's language.** Local, sensory, one word: night, limewash, smoke, peel, stone, in the brand's own language. Names are part of the voice. Show the meaning under each swatch.
4. **Test them together.** Albers: a colour is never seen alone. Test every pair as text on background and as a small shape on a large field.
   - Contrast: body text ≥ 7:1 is ideal and 4.5:1 is the minimum. Secondary text on the dark base ≥ 4.5:1. Mid-greys on a near-black base usually fail: lighten them until they separate.
   - Make a darker version of the secondary colour for small text on the light background.
   - Check the palette in greyscale: the roles must still separate.
   - Check common colour blindness (protan, deutan) for any pair that carries meaning.
5. **Print reality.** Give CMYK and a Pantone or nearest spot reference for the accent, and note anything that won't survive print (neon, deep gradients). If packaging is involved, ask about the substrate (kraft, foil, white board): kraft shifts every colour warm and dull.
6. **Write the rules.** Shares, what may sit on what, the one place the special colour is allowed, and one "never".

## Unorthodox strategies (pick one per brand, explain why)
| Strategy | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The place palette** | Every colour is sampled from one real street or building | A wall, the night sky, smoke, a vehicle's paint |
| **The ingredient palette** | Colours come from the product at different stages | Coffee: green bean → roast → crema → cup |
| **The archive plate** | One Wada or Werner combination, used faithfully and credited | A 1930s Japanese 3-colour combo for a tea brand |
| **The pigment palette** | Historic mineral and organic pigments with their stories | Lapis + orpiment + lac for a heritage craft brand |
| **The print-process palette** | Limited to what one press does | 2 riso inks + overprint = 3 colours, plus paper |
| **The time palette** | The palette shifts through a day or season, with one constant | Pages move from light to dark as the book goes on |
| **The one-loud rule** | Everything is neutral except one shocking colour in one place | A grey tech brand with a single safety-orange element |
| **The clash pair** | Two colours that "shouldn't" work, balanced by area | Teal × rose; purple × orange; lime glint on night |
| **Monochrome + tints** | One hue, 5 tints, plus black | A single-origin coffee brand in coffee tones |
| **The borrowed signal** | Colour from a public code: a road sign, a taxi, a hazard stripe, a school uniform | Taxi yellow + black |

## Gradients and specials
- A gradient belongs to one job only (usually the logo). Never use it for backgrounds or type.
- Add grain (±4% noise) to any large gradient so it doesn't band.
- Foil, spot UV or fluorescent inks are a print special: list them as specials with a flat fallback colour.

## The colour pages in the book
1. The palette as proportional bars (sized by share), with name, meaning, HEX / RGB / CMYK.
2. Light and dark versions of the same page.
3. The special (gradient or foil) and its single permitted use.
4. Pairings: Aa tiles for each allowed text/background pair, with the contrast ratio in mono.

## Master practices (see `../nazar-core/references/masters.md`)
- **Albers:** approve colours only on their real neighbours and real substrate, never as lone swatches.
- **Itten:** balance by area. The shares are part of the palette, not decoration.
- **Aicher (Munich 1972):** a palette chosen by what it *leaves out* can be the most memorable. Consider banning the category's default colour.
- **Sharp & Romaniuk:** one ownable colour, used everywhere for years, is a distinctive asset. Consistency beats richness.

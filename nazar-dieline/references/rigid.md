# Rigid and luxury boxes

A rigid (set-up) box is built, not folded: greyboard (chipboard) panels, wrapped in printed or specialty paper, delivered erected. It costs more to make and to ship (it doesn't ship flat, unless it's a collapsible rigid), and it's chosen for weight, crisp edges and the opening ritual.

## Construction
| Part | Starting values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Greyboard | ≈ 1.5–2 mm for small and medium boxes, 2.5–3 mm for large [SRC] | Density matters as much as thickness |
| Wrap paper | ≈ 120–150 gsm common [SRC] | Coated, uncoated, textured, cloth, leatherette, foil board |
| Turn-in | ≈ 12–20 mm (engine default 15) | The wrap folds over the top edge and shows **inside**: art must carry over the edge |
| Corner tabs | ≈ 10–15 mm | Wrap the corners before the end wraps cover them |
| Corners | Stay-taped square corners, or V-grooved (mitred) walls for sharp edges | V-groove gives the crisp "no visible board" corner |

The engine's `rigid` style draws the greyboard tray blank (V-groove creases, no bleed, since it's unprinted) and the wrap paper with turn-ins, double creases over the board edge, and corner tabs. Designers working from flat artwork often miss the turn-in allowance, the overlap at inside corners and the graphic offsets for wrapping around board [SRC]. That's why the wrap is drawn as its own piece.

## Common rigid styles
- **Lift-off lid** (telescope): lid and base; full or partial telescope. The lid's height sets the reveal.
- **Shoulder (neck) box:** a collar inside the base shows as a contrast band between lid and base. A premium signal, and a place for colour.
- **Hinged lid with magnetic closure:** magnets set in cavities in the board, under the wrap. A cavity cut too shallow shows a bump; too deep and the pull weakens [SRC].
- **Book box:** a hinged cover that opens like a book; the spine carries the name.
- **Drawer (matchbox) box:** a sleeve plus a tray; add a ribbon or thumb notch pull.
- **Collapsible (foldable) rigid:** ships flat, locks with magnets: freight savings for e-commerce gifting.
- **Clamshell, tube (round) and hexagonal** set-ups for special products.

## Inserts
EVA foam and velvet are premium but hard to recycle; card and paperboard fitments, moulded pulp, and folded corrugated inserts are better on recycling. Design the cavity from the product's real size + clearance, with finger cut-outs for lifting.

## The unboxing sequence (design it like a film)
1. **Exterior:** the first look. Embossing, foil, texture, maybe nothing but the mark.
2. **Opening resistance:** the lid's air-cushion slide, magnet click, ribbon pull, seal sticker. Make it deliberate.
3. **Reveal:** what you see first inside: a message under the lid, tissue, a card, the product face up.
4. **Lift:** how the product comes out (a ribbon lift, a finger notch, a tray).
5. **Second life:** what the box becomes (a storage box, a frame, a keepsake).
Script it in five frames on paper before choosing finishes. Every finish has to earn its place in the sequence.

## Production notes
- Wrap paper grain parallel to the long side, to limit curl and lifting.
- Specialty papers: ask for the wrap paper's printability (some don't take foil or fine type).
- Lead times are longer (hand assembly in many plants), and MOQs and freight are higher. Quote early.
- Approve a **physical pre-production sample** (white and printed); photograph it for reorders.

# Special requirements: tamper evidence, child resistance, braille, food contact, claims

None of these are design choices to improvise. The client (and their regulatory or QA team) owns the requirement and the evidence; we design the structure to meet it and leave room for it on the dieline.

## Tamper evidence (TE)
- Structural options: perforated tear strips, tear-off tabs that can't be re-closed, glued closures that show damage when opened, frangible bridges, TE labels or seals across the tuck, shrink bands.
- Carton designs can build TE into the structure itself (double locks, tear and grid perforations, glue-free tear strips) [SRC].
- Regulators may require TE alongside child resistance for some products (the Australian TGA code is a readable example) [SRC]. Check the market's rules.
- Design so the evidence is **visible at a glance on shelf** and the opening still feels good for the honest buyer.

## Child resistance (CR)
- Reclosable CR packaging is certified to standards such as **ISO 8317** (EU) and **16 CFR §1700.20** (US), and CR cartons exist that are certified to both [SRC]. Certification means testing with child and senior panels by an accredited lab.
- Structural approaches: push-and-slide drawers with hidden locks, squeeze-to-release tabs, tear-resistant board, key-operated lids [SRC].
- Never claim "child-resistant" without the test certificate for that exact structure and material.

## Braille on packaging (medicinal products)
- **EN ISO 17351** (2014, replacing EN 15823:2010) covers braille on medicinal packaging [SRC]. It sets a target embossed dot height of **about 0.20 mm**, with limits on how many readings may fall below lower thresholds [SRC].
- **Marburg Medium** is the usual spacing reference: about 2.5 mm between dots in a cell, about 6.0 mm cell to cell, about 10.0 mm line to line (vendor figures; check against the standard) [SRC].
- The marketing authorisation holder decides the spacing convention and text per market [SRC].
- Dieline impact: braille needs a **flat panel free of print-critical elements** behind it, away from creases and glue; it's embossed in the die-cutting step, so the die-maker places it. Mark the braille zone on the dieline and keep the artwork's small type off the back of it.

## Food contact
- Low-migration inks and coatings, or a functional barrier, are standard for food contact; the converter supplies the compliance documents [SRC: see nazar-packaging].
- Grease-resistant board, wet-strength board and freezer board change the creasing and the print: test them.
- Never put unfinished recycled board in direct contact with fatty or dry foods without a barrier: ask for migration data.

## Sustainability claims
- "Recyclable", "compostable", "plastic-free" and "made from recycled content" must be true for the **whole pack as sold, in that market** (windows, laminates, magnets, foam and glue all count). The client supplies the evidence; the structure is designed so the claim holds.
- Design for separation: windows that tear out, sleeves with tear strips, inserts in one material.
- Mono-material and right-sizing usually beat clever materials.

## 1b. Specific systems and techniques (not just "study an archive")
Generic advice to "study an archive" produces generic palettes. These are real, named, checkable instead:

- **Chevreul's law came from a dyeing complaint, not armchair theory.** As director of dyeing at the Gobelins tapestry works from 1824, Chevreul was told a black dye was defective. Testing proved the dye was sound — the black only *looked* weak and reddish next to deep blue/purple neighbours. That's simultaneous contrast: a colour shifts toward its neighbour's complement, in both hue and darkness. *De la loi du contraste simultané des couleurs* (1839; English 1854 as *The Principles of Harmony and Contrast of Colors*; new Margulis translation 2020). He taught it in public lectures at the Gobelins through the 1850s; Delacroix took notes from them and passed the method to younger painters [V].
- **Munsell notation still runs textile matching, and it disagrees with instrument colour.** SCOTDIC (merging Japan's and France's textile colour standards) is used by 8,000+ textile companies on Munsell's hue/value/chroma notation. A 2019 study found its value axis tracks CIELAB lightness closely but hue and chroma only moderately — a real, practical warning before trusting a Munsell-coded swatch against a CIELAB/OKLCH print proof [V].
- **Rogan art (Kutch, Gujarat), a stronger reference than generic ajrak:** castor oil boiled 12+ hours into a thick gum, mixed with ground mineral pigment, drawn onto cloth with a metal stylus ("kalam") that never touches the fabric — the artist stretches the paste into a fine thread by hand, sometimes mirroring the design onto an unpainted half without a mirror. Practised almost solely by the Khatri family of Nirona, Gujarat. No chemistry study found; dates are disputed across craft sources (300 vs 400+ years), so don't quote one [V, journalism-level sourcing only].
- **Pigment identification, the real method:** XRF mapping first (fast, non-destructive, flags candidate elements), Raman spectroscopy second to confirm only the ambiguous points (Heritage Science, 2018, DOI 10.1186/s40494-018-0217-y). On the Great Mongol Shahnama, this workflow confirmed lazurite — ground lapis — in all 14 folios studied, with indigo and azurite also present in mixtures (Matthew Clarke study, via Washington Conservation Guild). Orpiment needs no advanced instrument at all: it's identifiable under plain microscopy by its striated particles (Cambridge JRAS, "A Palette for a Prince") [V].
- **Pantone's Color of the Year has no published formula.** Pantone itself calls the process "highly subjective": an 8–12 person panel discusses trends (entertainment, art, socio-economic conditions) continuously, meeting twice a year, with no public criteria document. Cite it as an example of institutionalised colour-calling, never as a method to imitate [V].

## 2. Pakistani and South Asian palettes (strongest for Nazar)
- **Ajrak (Sindh):** a resist-dyed process — sources disagree on the stage count (14 to 21 across different accounts), so don't quote one as settled. Consistently reported: cloth is pre-soaked in oil and bundled for roughly two weeks so oil permeates every fibre; the resist paste ("Kharrh") is rice paste, alum, molasses, Fuller's earth, fennel and gum; myrobalan (harde fruit) powder is the mordant; black areas are stamped with a filler block, then the cloth goes into an indigo vat, then alizarin for red; finished cloth is spread on riverbanks and intermittently wetted, and that wet/dry cycling itself bleaches the white and deepens the other colours [V, craft-source level, no conservation paper found]. Sindh's old reputation as an indigo source likely gives "ajrak" its root (Arabic for blue). Caveat: many producers now use synthetic dyes, so don't claim an all-natural palette without checking the specific maker.
- **Truck art (phool patti):** saturated, mirror work, "disco" energy. **No documented fixed palette.** Durriya Kazi's 1998 paper "Decorated Trucks of Pakistan" (presented at a Leeuwenhorst conference; on her own blog) [V] is the real citable source — her point is that 1930s–40s trucks were single-colour-coated, with today's dominant saturated schemes emerging later. Sample real trucks with calibrated photos; don't invent a fixed palette.
- **Kashi-kari tilework** (Wazir Khan Mosque, Lahore; Thatta): cobalt, turquoise, ochre, green [K]. Multani and Hala blue pottery: cobalt and turquoise on white [UNVERIFIED specifics].
- **Mughal miniature pigments:** lapis lazuli, orpiment, cinnabar, gold; later Indian yellow; lac red [V] — see the pigment-identification method above for how these are actually confirmed, not assumed. Sample from open-access museum images (The Met CC0; Smithsonian NMAA), never by paraphrase.
- Traditional pigments: indigo, kumkum (turmeric + lime), hingul (cinnabar) [V popular source].

## 3. How strong studios build palettes
- Own one colour: Mailchimp's Cavendish Yellow (Collins, 2018); Tiffany Blue; Cadbury purple (fought in court) [K].
- Wide palettes run on pairing rules (Collins for Dropbox, 2017); deliberately abrasive ones break taste on purpose (Wolff Olins, London 2012) [K].
- Pentagram: palettes come from the client's own material (archive, building, product) [K].
- Made Good's brand-guideline studies of Schumacher (blue/terracotta) and Rubelli (teal/bronze) both explicitly frame the archive-derived palette as an *abstract homage* to the house's textile archive, not a literal colour-match — an honest model for "sourced from the client's own material" without overclaiming authenticity [V, editorial-level sourcing].

## 4. Technical checks
- **OKLCH/OKLab** (Ottosson 2020; CSS Color 4) are perceptually even. Ramps step L at a fixed hue and lower C at the extremes. Tools: oklch.com, Evil Martians' explainer.
- **WCAG 2.2:** 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text (24 px, or 18.66 px bold), 3:1 UI and graphics.
- **APCA:** its place in WCAG 3 is unresolved — as of April 2026 the draft's contrast algorithm is still marked "undetermined," APCA was flagged for possible removal back in 2023 and no return is confirmed; WCAG 3 itself is still a Working Draft, realistically years from finalisation (2028–2030) [V, Adrian Roselli, Apr 2026]. There is no official Lc threshold to cite. Use APCA only as a supplementary check on light-on-dark and thin type; **WCAG 2.2 is still the compliance standard.**
- **Colour blindness:** about 1 in 12 men, 1 in 200 women. Simulate with Chrome DevTools, Coblis or Sim Daltonism. Never use red/green alone for meaning; check in greyscale.

## 5. Screen → print
- sRGB/P3 are additive; CMYK is subtractive with a smaller gamut, so vivid greens, cyans, oranges and violets drop out.
- Uncoated stock dulls colour: specify Pantone C **and** U. Use the printer's ICC profile (FOGRA39/51, GRACoL). Total ink about 300% or less; rich black only on large areas.
- Pakistan practice [UNVERIFIED]: many printers don't work to profiles. Ask for wet or machine proofs, give Pantone references, press-check the first run. Use spot or fluorescent inks for truck-art saturation.

## 6. AI palette tells and avoidance
Tells:
- purple-to-blue gradients,
- neon cyan or magenta on near-black with glow,
- teal and orange,
- every hue at equal lightness (generator rainbows),
- pure #000/#FFF with one stock "brand blue".

Avoidance:
- start from a named source,
- tint the neutrals,
- use unequal proportions,
- mix lightness and chroma on purpose,
- keep one earthy friction colour,
- use gradients only from a real reference (dye bleed, sky at a stated hour),
- run the Albers relativity test.
